1. URBAN WATER SOURCES
====================

PRIORITY ORDER (Safety):

1. BOTTLED WATER (Stores, vending)
   - Expiration date meaningless if sealed
   - Check seal integrity
   - Clear > colored containers
   - Location: Stores, warehouses, gyms

2. WATER HEATERS (Buildings)
   - 40-80 gallons per unit
   - Usually clean if building recent
   - Drain valve at bottom
   - Multi-story buildings = multiple units

Access:
   - Basement/utility rooms
   - Turn off power first
   - Open pressure relief valve (top)
   - Open drain valve (bottom) with hose

3. TOILET TANKS (Not bowl!)
   - Tank holds 1.5-3 gallons
   - Relatively clean if unused
   - Avoid if chemical cleaners present
   - ALWAYS purify before drinking

4. SWIMMING POOLS
   - Thousands of gallons
   - Chlorine = pre-purified (partially)
   - Still filter before drinking
   - Last resort: industrial cooling towers

5. RAINWATER COLLECTION
   - Gutters and downspouts
   - Tarps between buildings
   - Container on roof access
   - First rain = flush system (dirty)

6. PIPES (Risky)
   - Cold water pipes may have residual
   - Open highest faucet (release pressure)
   - Open lowest faucet (collect)
   - Likely minimal yield

7. RIVERS/STREAMS
   - Urban waterways = HIGHLY contaminated
   - Chemical runoff, sewage
   - Multiple filtration + boiling essential
   - Absolute last resort

PURIFICATION (Critical in cities):

Method 1: Boiling
- Rolling boil: 1 minute (sea level)
- 3 minutes if above 2000m altitude
- Kills all pathogens
- Doesn't remove chemicals

Method 2: Chemical
- Bleach (5.25% sodium hypochlorite)
  * 8 drops per gallon
  * Wait 30 minutes
  * Smell should be chlorine
- Iodine tablets (follow instructions)

Method 3: Filtration
- Commercial filter (Sawyer, LifeStraw)
- DIY filter:
  * Container with hole in bottom
  * Layer: cloth → sand → charcoal → gravel → cloth
  * Slow drip through layers
  * Still boil after filtering!

Method 4: UV (if available)
- SteriPEN or sunlight + clear bottle
- 6 hours direct sunlight in PET bottle
- Only if water clear (pre-filter)

SIGNS OF BAD WATER:
⚠️ Oil sheen (gasoline, chemicals)
⚠️ Strong chemical smell
⚠️ Thick algae growth
⚠️ Dead animals nearby
⚠️ Foam (detergent runoff)

====================
2. FOOD ACQUISITION
====================

SCAVENGING LOCATIONS (Priority):

1. GROCERY STORES

   Shelf-Stable (months to years):
   - Canned goods (inspect for rust/bulging)
   - Dry pasta, rice (airtight = indefinite)
   - Honey (never expires)
   - Salt, sugar, spices
   - Peanut butter (sealed)
   - Powdered milk
   - Hardtack, crackers

   Avoid:
   - Refrigerated (spoiled within days)
   - Fresh produce (already rotting)
   - Bakery items (mold quickly)

   Safety:
   - Check dates (guideline, not absolute)
   - Bulging cans = BOTULISM (deadly)
   - Smell test (if opens, smells off = discard)

2. VENDING MACHINES

   Access:
   - Look for key slots (universal keys exist)
   - OR shimmy/tilt (products fall)
   - OR destructive (break glass) - LOUD

   Contents:
   - Chips, candy (high calorie, long shelf life)
   - Nuts (protein, fats)
   - Energy bars
   - Soft drinks (sugar, hydration)

3. WAREHOUSES

   Industrial areas = massive stockpiles
   - Food distribution centers
   - Costco/Sam's Club (bulk)
   - Restaurant supply warehouses
   - Shipping containers (ports)

   Security:
   - Usually fenced
   - Loading docks = weak point
   - Roof access possible
   - Bring bags/cart (volume)

4. RESTAURANTS (Commercial Kitchens)

   Dry storage areas:
   - 50lb bags rice, flour, beans
   - Industrial cans (#10 size)
   - Oils, condiments (sealed)
   - Freezers (spoiled without power)

5. HOSPITALS/SCHOOLS

   Cafeterias:
   - Similar to restaurants
   - Often overlooked by initial looters
   - Vending machines numerous

6. OFFICE BUILDINGS

   Break rooms:
   - Vending machines (each floor)
   - Employee food stashes (desks)
   - Candy bowls, snack drawers

7. HOMES (Private Residences)

   Pantries:
   - Varies widely
   - Suburban homes > apartments
   - Check basements (storage)

   Ethical consideration:
   - Someone's home = last resort
   - May be occupied (dangerous)

8. DUMPSTER DIVING (Pre-Collapse)

   Behind grocery stores:
   - Throw out near-expiration (still good)
   - Produce with blemishes (edible)
   - Dented cans (fine if no bulge/leak)

   Timing:
   - Night after closing
   - Just after restocking days

   Safety:
   - Inspect carefully
   - Wash everything
   - Cook thoroughly

URBAN HUNTING/TRAPPING:

Pigeons:
- Abundant in cities
- Trapping: Box trap with food bait
- Nesting areas: Bridges, abandoned buildings
- Preparation: Pluck, gut, boil (disease risk)

Rats:
- LAST RESORT (disease vectors)
- Snap traps or improvised
- Cook VERY thoroughly (160°F+)
- Remove all organs carefully
- High disease risk (leptospirosis, hantavirus)

Fish (if urban waterway clean):
- Unlikely due to pollution
- Improvised rod: Stick + line + hook
- Bait: Insects, small food scraps

Squirrels:
- Parks, trees
- Slingshot or trap
- Better than rats (less disease)

EDIBLE URBAN PLANTS:

Dandelions:
- Entire plant edible
- Leaves (salad), roots (roast for coffee substitute)
- Flowers (wine, fritters)
- Common in parks, yards

Plantain (not banana):
- Broad leaf weed
- Young leaves edible raw
- Older leaves cook like spinach

Purslane:
- Succulent weed
- High omega-3
- Leaves and stems raw or cooked

Acorns:
- Oak trees (parks)
- Leach tannins (boil multiple times)
- Grind into flour

⚠️ NEVER eat unknown plants (poisoning risk)
⚠️ Avoid plants near roads (contamination)

3. SHELTER & CONCEALMENT
====================

LOCATION PRIORITIES:

1. ABANDONED BUILDINGS

   Best:
   - Upper floors (less traffic)
   - Multiple exits (fire escapes)
   - Obscured windows (concealment)
   - Structural integrity (check roof, floors)

   Avoid:
   - Ground floor (easy access = more people)
   - Single entry (trap)
   - Known squats (violence common)

   Security:
   - Barricade entry behind you
   - Conceal that floor is occupied
   - Quiet at all times
   - Rotate locations

2. PARKING GARAGES (Underground)

   Advantages:
   - Climate controlled (cool/warm)
   - Protection from elements
   - Multiple levels = hiding spots
   - Less visible than street

   Spots:
   - Stairwells (semi-private)
   - Storage rooms
   - Mechanical areas

3. SUBWAY/METRO TUNNELS

   Pros:
   - Underground = hidden
   - Passages between areas
   - Alcoves for maintenance

   Cons:
   - Flooding risk
   - Darkness (light needed)
   - Rats, disease
   - Other people (gangs)

   Safety:
   - Never go deep without light
   - Mark exit routes
   - Avoid third rail (electrocution if powered)

4. ROOFTOPS

   Access:
   - Fire escapes
   - Interior stairwells
   - Ladders

   Benefits:
   - High ground = visibility
   - Less foot traffic
   - Rain collection easy
   - Signaling possible

   Risks:
   - Exposure to weather
   - Visible from other roofs/buildings
   - Limited escape routes

5. SHIPPING CONTAINERS

   Locations:
   - Ports
   - Industrial areas
   - Rail yards

   Setup:
   - Insulate (metal = extreme temps)
   - Ventilation critical (suffocation)
   - Conceal entry
   - Lock from inside

6. SEWERS (Extreme Last Resort)

   Pros:
   - Hidden
   - Extensive network
   - Water source (requires purification!)

   Cons:
   - Toxic gases (H2S, methane)
   - Disease (every pathogen imaginable)
   - Flooding (rain = death)
   - Darkness
   - Rats

   Survival:
   - Gas detector if possible
   - Never alone
   - Rope/chalk trail (don't get lost)
   - High ground for sleep (flooding)

CONCEALMENT TECHNIQUES:

Camouflage:
- Blend with environment
- Tarp/cardboard over "home"
- Look like random trash
- Avoid patterns (symmetry = human)

Noise Discipline:
- No talking loudly
- Soft-soled shoes
- Secure gear (no rattling)
- Nighttime movement only if needed

Light Discipline:
- No fires (smoke visible miles)
- Flashlight: Red filter (less visible)
- Candles: Deep in structure only
- Blackout windows

Smell Discipline:
- Cooking = attracts people
- Human waste = disease + smell
- Bury or remove far from camp

4. URBAN NAVIGATION
====================

WITHOUT GPS/MAPS:

Direction Finding:

Sun:
- Rises East, Sets West (general)
- Shadow stick method:
  1. Place stick vertical
  2. Mark shadow tip
  3. Wait 15min, mark again
  4. Line = East-West

Stars (Northern Hemisphere):
- Polaris (North Star)
- Find Big Dipper → Follow outer cup stars → 5x distance = Polaris

Moss:
- MYTH (unreliable in cities)
- Buildings shade all sides

Landmarks:
- Tall buildings = reference points
- Rivers (follow downstream = civilization)
- Highways/rail = follow to exits

ROUTE PLANNING:

Avoid:
- Main streets (exposed, populated)
- Choke points (bridges, tunnels = ambush)
- Known danger zones (mark mentally)

Prefer:
- Alleys and backstreets
- Parallel routes to main roads
- Multiple path options
- High ground routes (roofs if connected)

Movement:

Daylight:
- More visibility = more risk
- Faster travel
- Easier navigation
- Higher encounter chance

Night:
- Concealment better
- Slower, dangerous (trip hazards)
- Need light source (gives away position)
- Less people (but more dangerous people)

Group:
- Quieter than seems (discipline needed)
- More security
- More visible
- Slower decision-making

Solo:
- Complete stealth possible
- All decisions yours
- No backup (injury = death)
- Faster movement

CROSSING OBSTACLES:

Fences:
- Chain link: Climb (toe holds)
- Barbed wire: Thick blanket over top
- Razor wire: AVOID (too dangerous)

Walls:
- Running start + jump + pull
- Boost (teammate)
- Stack objects (pallets, crates)

Rivers/Canals:
- Bridges (may be watched)
- Swim (current danger, hypothermia)
- Improvised raft (dangerous)

5. ENTRY TECHNIQUES
====================

DOORS:

Knob Locks (No Deadbolt):
- Credit card shimming
- Bypass latch with thin rigid tool
- Success: 60% on older doors

Deadbolts:
- Lockpicking (see other documents)
- Bumping
- Drilling (destructive, loud)
- OR find another entry

Kicking/Shouldering:
- Hit near lock (not center)
- Multiple strong kicks
- LOUD (attention)
- Damages door (obvious entry)

Barricaded Doors:
- Check hinges (exterior = remove pins)
- Alternate entry
- Don't force (trap possible)

WINDOWS:

Breaking Glass:
- Corner strike (less noise)
- Wrapped hand/tool (muffle)
- Remove remaining shards (cuts)
- LOUD (attention)

Sliding Windows:
- Lift off track (if not secured)
- Slim jim between frame

Locked Windows:
- Thin blade to flip latch
- Cut glass with glass cutter (quiet)
- Break as last resort

ALTERNATIVE ENTRIES:

Fire Escapes:
- External access (climb from ground)
- Upper floor = safer, less used

Basement Windows:
- Often overlooked
- Smaller = tight squeeze
- Check for barring first

Ventilation:
- Large ducts = possible entry
- Bring light
- May lead to dead ends
- Noise travels in ducts

Roof Access:
- Adjacent buildings
- Climb exterior (drainpipes, ledges)
- Drop through skylight/hatch

6. VEHICLE ACCESS
====================

OPENING LOCKED CAR:

Slim Jim (Pre-2000 vehicles):
- Slide between window and weather stripping
- Hook vertical rod mechanism
- Pull up (unlocks)
- Modern cars: Electronic locks (doesn't work)

Wedge + Rod Method:
- Wedge: Inflate in door gap (top corner)
- Rod: Long stiff wire
- Reach interior unlock button
- Works on most vehicles
- Non-destructive

Tennis Ball Myth:
- Pressure unlock: DOESN'T WORK
- Internet myth
- Waste of time

Breaking Window:
- Side windows: Safety glass (shatters completely)
- Aim: Bottom corner
- Tool: Spark plug ceramic (shatters easily)
- LOUD, obvious

HOT-WIRING (Pre-1990 Vehicles):

⚠️ Modern vehicles (post-2000): IMPOSSIBLE (immobilizer chips)

Classic Method (Pre-1990):

1. ACCESS STEERING COLUMN
   - Remove plastic cover (screws or pull hard)
   - Expose wire bundle

2. IDENTIFY WIRES
   - RED: Battery (always hot)
   - YELLOW/BROWN: Accessories
   - BLACK/WHITE: Ground
   - GREEN/PURPLE: Starter

   (Colors vary by make - test with multimeter if possible)

3. STRIP INSULATION
   - 1-2 inches from ends
   - Careful not to cut wire

4. CONNECT
   - Twist RED + YELLOW together
   - Dashboard lights = success
   - Touch GREEN to combined briefly (engine starts)
   - Remove GREEN immediately (starter disengages)
   - Drive with exposed wires

DANGERS:
⚡ Shock possible (12V low risk)
🔥 Short circuit = fire
🚨 Modern alarm systems
⚖️ ILLEGAL (grand theft auto)

Steering Lock:
- Most vehicles: Mechanical lock
- Forceful turn breaks mechanism (older)
- Modern: Electronic (very difficult)

FUEL:

Siphoning:
1. Tube into tank
2. Suck until fuel reaches mouth
3. SPIT (don't swallow!)
4. Quickly insert into container
5. Gravity flows

Anti-siphon screens:
- Modern vehicles have them
- Can push 1/4" tube through sometimes
- OR drill tank (permanent damage)

Fuel Priorities:
1. Vehicles (obviously)
2. Generators
3. Gas station pumps (manual override)

ALTERNATE TRANSPORT:

Bicycles:
- Faster than walking
- Silent
- No fuel needed
- Easy maintenance
- Lock pick bike locks (U-lock harder)

Motorcycles:
- Fuel efficient
- Maneuverability
- Loud (attention)
- Less storage

====================
7. COUNTER-SURVEILLANCE
====================

DETECTING BEING FOLLOWED:

Four Turns Test:
- Turn right four times (full circle)
- Same person/vehicle = following

Unusual Stops:
- Abrupt stop, window shopping
- Follower must also stop (reveals them)

Reflection:
- Car mirrors, store windows
- Observe without turning

Choke Point:
- Single entrance/exit location
- Enter, watch who follows

LOSING A TAIL:

Public Transit:
- Board train/bus
- Exit at last second (doors closing)
- Follower trapped or reveals themselves

Crowds:
- Duck into crowded area
- Change direction inside
- Exit different door

Quick Changes:
- Remove/add jacket
- Hat on/off
- Sunglasses
- Changes silhouette

Multiple Buildings:
- Enter store, exit back/side
- Through multiple connected shops
- Harder to follow unseen

HIDING:

Urban Camouflage:
- Homeless appearance (blend in)
- Worker appearance (hi-vis vest = invisible)
- Jogger (explains being in area)

Hiding Spots:
- Recessed doorways
- Dumpster enclosures
- Under vehicles (temporary)
- Inside stores (blend with customers)

OBSERVATION WITHOUT BEING SEEN:

Reflections:
- Building windows
- Vehicle mirrors
- Phone screen (fake call while watching)

Peripheral Vision:
- Don't stare directly
- Glance and look away
- Build mental map

Elevated Positions:
- Second floor windows
- Parking garages
- Overpasses

AVOIDING CAMERAS:

Modern City Reality:
- Cameras everywhere (impossible to avoid all)
- Facial recognition advancing
- License plate readers

Techniques (Limited effectiveness):
- Hood/hat + head down
- Sunglasses (daytime)
- Face mask (post-COVID = normal)
- Avoid main streets
- IR reflective clothing (some cameras)

====================
9. GANG/THREAT AVOIDANCE
====================

POST-COLLAPSE THREATS:

Desperate Individuals:
- Most common
- Unpredictable
- Looking for supplies
- May or may not be violent

Organized Gangs:
- Territory control
- Tax/toll systems
- More dangerous (numbers)
- Avoid territory

Marauders:
- Roving groups
- Pure violence/theft
- Most dangerous
- Avoid at all costs

Law Enforcement (Early):
- May still exist
- Overstretched
- Curfews possible
- Avoid confrontation

Military (Martial Law):
- Checkpoints
- Restricted zones
- Comply or avoid

TERRITORIAL AWARENESS:

Gang Signs:
- Graffiti markings
- Specific colors
- Symbols

Safe Passage:
- Pay toll if required (supplies)
- Don't challenge territory
- Move through quickly

Neutral Zones:
- Everyone needs resources
- Temporary truces
- Still dangerous

DEFENSIVE POSTURE:

Appear:
- Not worth robbing (homeless look)
- OR too dangerous to attack (armed, confident)
- NOT wealthy/well-supplied (target)

Travel:
- Groups safer (but louder)
- Vary routes
- Move during "safer" times
- Have exit plan always

Weapons:
- Visible deterrent vs concealed surprise
- Firearm (if legal/available)
- Knife (backup)
- Improvised (club, Molotov, etc.)

CONFLICT AVOIDANCE:

De-escalation:
- Comply if mugged (items replaceable, life isn't)
- Don't make eye contact (challenge)
- Back away slowly
- Hands visible (non-threatening)

When to Fight:
- Life threatened regardless
- Can't escape
- Defending others
- LAST RESORT

When to Run:
- Path available
- Can outrun (unburdened)
- No hostages
- PREFERRED OPTION

10. POWER & ELECTRICITY
====================

POWER GENERATION:

Solar (Scavenge panels):
- Rooftop panels (homes, businesses)
- Portable panels (camping stores)
- Charge batteries during day
- 12V system easiest

Gasoline Generators:
- Scavenge from hardware stores
- Fuel limited
- LOUD (attracts attention)
- Use sparingly

Car Battery:
- 12V DC power source
- Inverter = 110V AC
- Charge with alternator (running car)
- Limited capacity

Wind (Urban difficult):
- Rooftop small turbine
- Inconsistent in cities
- DIY possible (car alternator + blades)

Pedal Power:
- Bicycle + alternator
- Human-powered generation
- Charges batteries slowly

ELECTRICAL SOURCES:

Traffic Lights:
- Solar panels on top (many models)
- Battery backup inside box
- Access control box (locked)

Street Lights:
- Some solar-powered
- Battery inside pole base

Emergency Lights:
- Buildings have backup batteries
- Exit signs (small batteries)

Vehicles:
- Batteries (12V)
- Alternator (running = power)

IMPROVISED LIGHTING:

Candles:
- Scavenge stores, churches
- DIY: Fat/wax + wick
- Fire hazard (ventilation)

Oil Lamps:
- Container + wick + oil
- Cooking oil works
- Better than candles (brighter)

Chemical (Glow sticks):
- Single use
- 8-12 hours light
- No heat/fire

Shake Flashlight:
- Magnetic induction
- No batteries
- Low light output

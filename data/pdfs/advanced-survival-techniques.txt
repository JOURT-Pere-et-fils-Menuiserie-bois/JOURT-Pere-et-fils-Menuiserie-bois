1. PRIMITIVE FIRE METHODS
====================

BOW DRILL (Most Reliable):

Components:
- Bow: Curved branch, ~arm length
- Bowstring: Paracord, leather strip, plant fiber
- Spindle: Straight stick, ~pencil diameter, 8-12"
- Fireboard: Flat, dry softwood
- Socket: Hard wood with depression, fits in palm
- Tinder bundle: Very fine, dry material

Wood Selection:
- Spindle & Fireboard: Same wood (cedar, willow, cottonwood, yucca)
- Socket: Harder wood (oak, maple) OR stone with depression
- Avoid: Resinous woods (pine), wet wood

Technique:
1. Carve V-notch in fireboard (pie slice shape)
2. Start hole with spindle (no notch yet)
3. Burn in hole until smoking
4. Carve notch to center of hole
5. Place bark under notch (catch coal)
6. Bow drill rapidly with downward pressure
7. Smoke → Coal forms in notch
8. Carefully transfer coal to tinder bundle
9. Blow gently until flame

Common Failures:
- Too much/little pressure (Goldilocks zone)
- Wet wood or humidity
- Poor spindle/fireboard fit
- Not enough speed
- Coal forms but tinder too coarse

HAND DRILL (Difficult):

Components:
- Spindle: Straight stick, 18-24", ~pencil diameter
- Fireboard: Softwood
- No bow needed

Technique:
- Roll spindle between palms
- Downward pressure while spinning
- Very difficult (requires practice)
- Coal forms same as bow drill

Tips:
- Start high, hands move down as spinning
- Hands must move down spindle (continuous pressure)
- Speed critical (very fast)
- Best in dry climates

FIRE PLOW:

Method:
- Rub stick back/forth in groove on board
- Friction creates dust
- Dust heats, forms coal
- Push coal to tinder

Difficulty: Medium
Success rate: Lower than bow drill

FLINT & STEEL:

Materials:
- Flint, chert, or hard rock
- Steel striker (knife back works)
- Char cloth (cotton charred in sealed tin)

Process:
1. Strike steel on flint (shower of sparks)
2. Spark lands on char cloth (glows)
3. Place glowing char in tinder bundle
4. Blow to flame

Modern: Ferrocerium rod
- Much easier than flint
- Scrape with knife spine
- 3000°F sparks
- Works when wet
- Thousands of strikes

FIRE FROM FRICTION ALTERNATIVES:

Fire Saw:
- Bamboo split lengthwise
- Saw one piece on edge of other
- Friction creates coal

Pump Drill:
- Flywheel on spindle
- Cord wrapped, pumped up/down
- More complex, higher speed

2. ADVANCED SHELTER CONSTRUCTION
====================

DEBRIS HUT (Insulated, Solo):

Structure:
- Ridge pole: 7-10 feet, strong branch
- A-frame: Ridge pole at 45°
- Ribbing: Sticks leaned on both sides
- Debris: Leaves, grass, pine needles (3-4 feet thick!)
- Interior bedding: Dry leaves, pine needles

Dimensions:
- Just big enough to fit (body heat)
- ~3 feet wide, 6 feet long
- Entrance small (plug with debris)

Insulation:
- 3+ feet debris = R-value sufficient
- Interior bedding critical (ground cold)
- Waterproof outer layer (bark, tarp)

Temperature:
- Can maintain 20-30°F above outside
- Body heat trapped
- Condensation issue (ventilation balance)

LONG-TERM CABIN:

Site Selection:
- Near water (not too close - floods)
- High ground (drainage)
- South-facing (Northern Hemisphere - sun)
- Protected from wind
- Resources (wood, food, materials)

Foundation:
- Flat rock piers
- Log sills elevated (rot prevention)
- Level critical

Walls:
- Log cabin: Notched corners (saddle, dove tail)
- Wattle & daub: Woven sticks, mud plaster
- Cob: Clay/sand/straw mix
- Cordwood: Short logs in mortar

Roof:
- A-frame or shed style
- Ridge beam + rafters
- Bark shingles (overlap like scales)
- Thatch (grass, reeds)
- Sod (very heavy, good insulation)

Chinking (Seal gaps):
- Moss + mud
- Clay daub
- Oakum (tarred rope)

EARTHEN STRUCTURES:

Dugout:
- Excavate into hillside
- Roof with logs + earth
- Very insulated
- Risk: Collapse, water

Pit House:
- Dig pit 3-4 feet deep
- Roof over with A-frame
- Earth over roof
- Ancient design (works)

Adobe:
- Sun-dried mud bricks
- Clay + sand + straw
- Form in molds
- Stack when dry
- Mud mortar

SOD HOUSE:

Construction:
- Cut sod blocks (grass + roots + soil)
- Stack like bricks
- Slight inward lean (dome effect)
- Wooden roof frame
- Sod over roof

Benefits:
- Excellent insulation
- Fire resistant
- Durable (decades)
- Low material cost

Drawbacks:
- Very heavy
- Leaks if poor drainage
- Dark interior

3. HUNTING & TRAPPING
====================

DEADFALL TRAPS:

Figure-4 Trigger:
```
     [Weight]
        |
    [Vertical]
       /|
[Bait]/ |
     /[Diagonal]
    /   |
[Horizontal on ground]
```

- Carve notches to interlock
- Delicate balance
- Animal disturbs bait → collapse
- Rock/log kills animal
- Effective for small game

Paiute Deadfall:
- More sensitive than Figure-4
- Toggle stick trigger
- Harder to set but better

SNARES:

Simple Snare:
- Wire loop (picture wire, snare wire)
- Anchor to tree/stake
- Place in animal path
- Loop size: Fist-sized (rabbit), larger for deer
- Height: Bottom of animal's head

Spring Snare:
- Bent sapling provides force
- Trigger releases spring
- Lifts animal off ground (prevents escape)
- More complex but effective

Placement:
- Game trails (look for tracks, scat)
- Fence lines (animals follow)
- Water edges
- Between obstacles (natural funnel)

Legal: CHECK LAWS (Snaring illegal many places)

FISHING:

Primitive Methods:

Spear:
- 6-8 foot pole
- Sharpened point OR forked tines
- Aim low (refraction)
- Pin fish to bottom
- Still water works best

Gorge Hook:
- Straight stick sharpened both ends
- Bait tied to middle
- Fish swallows, turns sideways in throat
- Ancient but effective

Fish Trap:
- Funnel shape (easy in, hard out)
- Weave sticks/reeds
- Place in stream
- Check daily

Modern:

Yo-Yo Reel:
- Automatic fishing reel
- Set on bank
- Fish pulls, sets hook, reels in
- Can set multiple

Trot Line:
- Main line across water
- Multiple hooks on droplines
- Leave overnight
- Catfish effective

HUNTING:

Stalking:
- Move slowly (1 step per minute)
- Wind in face (scent away from animal)
- Freeze when animal looks
- Early morning/dusk (animals active)
- Camouflage + silence

Ambush:
- Find game trail, water source, feeding area
- Set up downwind
- Remain still
- Patience critical
- Tree stand (elevation advantage)

Tracking:
- Identify tracks (species, age, direction)
- Follow spoor (scat, broken vegetation)
- Morning = fresh tracks (dew shows)
- Don't skyline (ridge = visible)

BUTCHERING:

Field Dressing (Deer):
1. Hang by hind legs OR lay on back
2. Cut around anus/genitals (careful not to puncture)
3. Slice belly skin from anus to sternum
4. Cut muscle wall (don't puncture intestines)
5. Reach in, cut diaphragm
6. Pull out organs (save heart, liver if desired)
7. Wipe cavity clean
8. Cool carcass ASAP

Skinning:
- Cut around legs (joints)
- Peel hide (knife helps where tight)
- Work from legs toward center
- Save hide (tanning later)

Quartering:
- Remove legs at joints
- Separate back straps (along spine)
- Remove neck, ribs
- Trim fat, silver skin

Preservation:
- Hang in cool, dry place (aging, 1-2 weeks)
- Smoke/dry if no refrigeration
- Salt heavily
- Keep flies off (mesh bag)

4. FORAGING & WILD EDIBLES
====================

⚠️ RULE: 100% Positive ID or DON'T EAT

UNIVERSAL EDIBILITY TEST:

⚠️ LAST RESORT - Many plants deadly

1. Separate plant into parts
2. Smell (strong/acid = avoid)
3. Skin test: Rub on wrist, wait 15 min
4. Lip test: Touch to lips, wait 3 min
5. Tongue test: Touch to tongue, wait 15 min
6. Chew test: Chew small amount, wait 15 min
7. Swallow: Small amount, wait 5 hours
8. If no reaction: Eat 1/4 cup, wait 5 hours
9. If still OK: Edible (probably)

Time: 24+ hours per plant part
Risk: Still dangerous

COMMON EDIBLES (North America):

Dandelion (Taraxacum officinale):
- Entire plant edible
- Leaves: Salad (young), cooked (old, bitter)
- Roots: Roasted (coffee substitute)
- Flowers: Wine, fritters
- Very common, easy ID

Cattail (Typha):
- "Supermarket of the swamp"
- Roots: Starch (pound, wash, dry)
- Shoots: Young shoots like asparagus
- Pollen: Mix with flour
- Seed fluff: Tinder, insulation

Plantain (Plantago):
- Broadleaf weed
- Young leaves: Raw salad
- Older leaves: Cook like spinach
- Medicinal: Poultice for stings/bites

Acorns (Quercus):
- All oak species
- High tannins (bitter, toxic)
- Leach tannins: Boil, change water repeatedly (until not bitter)
- Grind into flour
- High calories

Pine (Pinus):
- Inner bark (cambium): Edible, carbs
- Needles: Tea (Vitamin C)
- Nuts (some species): High fat, protein

Berries:
- Aggregate berries (raspberry, blackberry): Generally safe
- Blue/black berries: 90% edible
- Red berries: 50% edible
- White/yellow berries: 10% edible (avoid!)

DEADLY PLANTS (Avoid):

Water Hemlock (Cicuta):
- EXTREMELY deadly
- Looks like carrot/parsnip
- White roots, purple streaks on stem
- Grows near water

Poison Hemlock (Conium):
- Killed Socrates
- Looks like wild carrot
- Purple spots on stem
- Musty smell

Foxglove (Digitalis):
- Heart toxin
- Beautiful purple flowers
- All parts deadly

Deadly Nightshade (Atropa):
- Black berries
- Extremely toxic
- Dilates pupils

MUSHROOMS:

⚠️ EXTREME CAUTION - Many deadly

Rules:
- Never eat LBM's ("Little Brown Mushrooms")
- Avoid: White gills, ring on stem, bulbous base (Amanita)
- Death Cap (Amanita phalloides) = DEADLY
- Destroying Angel (Amanita virosa) = DEADLY

Safe Mushrooms (with proper ID):
- Morels (Morchella): Honeycomb cap, hollow
- Puffballs (Lycoperdon): White inside (no gills)
- Chicken of the Woods (Laetiporus): Orange shelf fungus
- Lion's Mane (Hericium): White, spines

ALWAYS: 100% positive ID + field guide + expert confirmation

5. FOOD PRESERVATION
====================

SMOKING:

Cold Smoking (68-86°F):
- Preserves, doesn't cook
- 12-48 hours
- Smoke house OR DIY (pit + sticks)
- Wood: Hickory, apple, oak (NO pine/resin)

Hot Smoking (100-190°F):
- Cooks + preserves
- 4-8 hours
- Still need salt
- Eat sooner (days not months)

Process:
1. Brine meat (salt solution, 12-24 hours)
2. Rinse, pat dry
3. Air dry (pellicle forms)
4. Smoke (low, steady)
5. Cool completely
6. Store in cool, dry place

DRYING (Jerky):

Slicing:
- Thin strips (1/4 inch)
- Against grain = chewy
- With grain = tender

Marinade:
- Salt (critical for preservation)
- Optional: Spices, soy sauce
- 6-24 hours

Drying:
- Hang in sun (fly protection)
- Smoke house (low heat)
- Dehydrator (modern)
- Until brittle/leathery
- No moisture = long storage

SALTING:

Dry Cure:
- Cover meat completely with salt
- Draw out moisture
- 1 week per inch thickness
- Rinse before eating (VERY salty)

Brine:
- Saturated salt solution
- Submerge meat (weight down)
- 1-3 weeks
- Rinse well

Salt Ratios:
- Dry: 100% salt coverage
- Brine: 1 cup salt per gallon water (minimum)
- Sugar optional (flavor, not preservation)

FERMENTATION:

Sauerkraut:
- Shredded cabbage + 2% salt
- Pack tight (exclude air)
- Weight keeps submerged
- 1-4 weeks (room temp)
- Lactobacillus ferments
- Acidic = preserved

Kim Chi:
- Cabbage, radish, spices
- Similar to sauerkraut
- More complex flavors
- Traditional Korean

Pickling:
- Vinegar brine (5% acetic acid minimum)
- Salt, sugar, spices
- Boil vegetables in brine
- Seal in jars
- Acid = preservation

ROOT CELLARING:

Conditions:
- Cool (32-40°F)
- High humidity (80-95%)
- Dark
- Ventilation

Storage:
- Potatoes, carrots, beets, turnips
- Apples (separate - release ethylene gas)
- Don't wash (remove soil when using)
- Check regularly (remove rotten)

Construction:
- Underground (natural cooling)
- Buried barrel
- Basement corner (coolest)
- Styrofoam cooler in ground

6. PRIMITIVE TOOLS & WEAPONS
====================

STONE TOOLS:

Flint Knapping:

Process:
1. Select stone (flint, obsidian, chert)
2. Percussion flaking (large flakes)
3. Pressure flaking (fine edges)
4. Hafting (attach to handle)

Tools:
- Hammerstone (hard rock, round)
- Antler billet (controlled flaking)
- Leather pad (pressure flaking)

Safety:
- Glasses (sharp chips fly)
- Gloves
- Away from people

Simple Tools:
- Hand axe (no handle, hold rock)
- Scraper (hide processing)
- Blade (cutting)
- Arrowhead/spear point

HAFTING (Attach blade to handle):

Method:
1. Split end of stick
2. Insert blade
3. Lash with cordage/sinew
4. Seal with pine pitch + charcoal (strong glue)

Pitch Glue:
- Collect pine sap
- Heat gently (don't boil)
- Mix with charcoal powder (50/50)
- Apply while warm
- Very strong when cool

BONE TOOLS:

Awl (Punch holes):
- Long bone (deer leg)
- Break, sharpen on stone
- Very hard, durable
- Leatherworking essential

Needle:
- Small bone, bird bone
- Carve, drill eye
- Sewing

Fish Hook:
- Carved bone, gorge style
- Modern: Safety pin shape

AXE:

Stone Axe:
- Grooved stone head
- Withe binding (green wood, shrinks when dry)
- OR antler handle (socket)

Tomahawk:
- Metal head (scavenged)
- Wooden handle
- Wedge + lashing

Uses:
- Chopping wood
- Weapon
- Tool making

BOW:

Wood Selection:
- Yew (best)
- Osage orange
- Hickory
- White oak
- Ash

Construction:
1. Select straight branch/sapling (5-6 feet)
2. Remove bark
3. Carve (thicker center, tapering to tips)
4. Tillering (equal flex both limbs)
5. Notch tips (string attachment)
6. String (bowstring, dacron, sinew)

Draw Weight:
- 40-60 lbs (hunting)
- 20-30 lbs (small game, practice)
- Length: Height minus 6 inches

ARROWS:

Shaft:
- Straight shoots (dogwood, willow, bamboo)
- Dry 6+ months (warp prevention)
- Straighten over heat
- Spine (flex) must match bow

Fletching:
- Feathers (turkey, goose)
- 3 feathers, evenly spaced
- Glue + bind
- Stabilizes flight

Nock:
- Notch in end (fits bowstring)
- Shallow V-shape

Point:
- Stone (knapped)
- Metal (scavenged, sharpened)
- Fire-hardened wood (primitive)
- Bone

7. CORDAGE & ROPE MAKING
====================

PLANT FIBERS:

Sources:
- Nettle (very strong)
- Dogbane
- Milkweed
- Inner bark (basswood, cedar)
- Yucca leaves
- Cattail leaves

Processing:
1. Strip fibers from plant
2. Ret (soak in water, bacteria breaks down)
3. OR pound to separate
4. Dry fibers
5. Comb/separate fine strands

REVERSE WRAP METHOD:

Process:
1. Take two fiber bundles
2. Twist both clockwise (same direction)
3. Wrap around each other counter-clockwise
4. Maintain tension
5. Add fiber when running short (splice)
6. Continue indefinitely

Result:
- Very strong
- Doesn't unravel
- Fine thread to thick rope possible

SINEW:

Source:
- Back strap (along spine) of deer, elk
- Legs tendons

Processing:
1. Remove sinew (fresh kill)
2. Dry completely
3. Pound to separate fibers
4. Very fine, strong fibers

Uses:
- Bow backing (increases draw weight)
- Sewing thread
- Lashing
- Very durable

RAWHIDE:

Process:
1. Fresh hide (deer, cow, etc.)
2. Soak in water (hair slips)
3. Scrape off hair
4. Stretch and dry (do NOT tan)
5. Hard when dry

Uses:
- Cut into strips (lashing)
- Wet rawhide, wrap, shrinks tight when dry
- Drum heads
- Containers

BARK:

Basswood/Cedar:
- Strip bark in spring (sap flowing)
- Inner bark (bast) is cordage
- Outer bark (strips for baskets)

Birch Bark:
- Waterproof
- Containers, canoes, roofing
- Horizontal lines (lenticels) = birch

8. NAVIGATION WITHOUT INSTRUMENTS
====================

CELESTIAL NAVIGATION:

Sun:
- Rises East, Sets West (general)
- Noon: Highest point, due South (Northern Hemisphere)
- Shadow stick: Shortest shadow = noon = North-South line

Shadow Tip Method:
1. Vertical stick in ground
2. Mark shadow tip (first mark)
3. Wait 15+ minutes
4. Mark new shadow tip (second mark)
5. Line between marks = East-West (first mark = West)

Stars (Northern Hemisphere):

Polaris (North Star):
1. Find Big Dipper
2. Follow outer edge of "cup"
3. Extend ~5x that distance
4. Bright star = Polaris
5. Polaris = True North (within 1°)

Orion's Belt:
- Rises due East
- Sets due West
- Visible worldwide
- Three stars in line

Southern Cross (Southern Hemisphere):
- Pointer to South
- Extend long axis 4.5x
- Point to horizon = South

Moon:
- Waxing (growing): Sets after midnight (West)
- Waning (shrinking): Rises after midnight (East)
- Full: Rises at sunset (East), sets at sunrise (West)

NATURAL NAVIGATION:

Moss:
- MYTH (unreliable)
- Grows on moist side (can be any direction)
- Don't rely on moss alone

Trees:
- More branches on South side (Northern Hemisphere) - GENERAL
- Unreliable (individual trees vary)
- Forest as whole = slight trend

Prevailing Winds:
- Sand dunes, snow drifts shaped by wind
- Regional knowledge required
- Consistent direction

Landmarks:
- Distant mountains, towers
- Rivers (flow downhill, to ocean)
- Power lines (usually North-South, East-West)

MAP & COMPASS (If available):

Map Reading:
- Orient map (North on map = North in reality)
- Identify landmarks
- Triangulation (bearings from 2+ points)

Compass Use:
- Magnetic North ≠ True North (declination)
- Adjust for declination (maps show amount)
- Sight bearing, walk straight line
- Re-check periodically

Improvised Compass:
- Magnetize needle (stroke with magnet, silk)
- Float on water (leaf, cork)
- Points North-South
- Mark which end is North (Sun direction)

9. WEATHER PREDICTION
====================

CLOUDS:

Cirrus (High, Wispy):
- 24-48 hours before storm
- "Mare's tails"
- Wind direction indicator

Cumulus (Fluffy, White):
- Fair weather
- If grow vertical (cumulonimbus) = thunderstorm
- Dark bottom = rain coming

Stratus (Low, Gray Sheet):
- Overcast
- Light rain/drizzle
- Stable weather

Cumulonimbus (Towering):
- Thunderstorm imminent
- Anvil top = severe
- Lightning, hail, tornado possible
- Seek shelter immediately

BAROMETRIC PRESSURE:

Rising:
- Improving weather
- Clear skies coming
- Stable conditions

Falling:
- Worsening weather
- Storm approaching
- Faster fall = more severe

Rapid Fall:
- Severe weather (hurricane, blizzard)
- Batten down

INDICATORS:

Animals:
- Birds flying low = rain coming (pressure)
- Cows lying down (MYTH)
- Frogs loud = rain (moisture)
- Ants building high mounds = flood

Smoke:
- Rises straight = high pressure (good weather)
- Hangs low = low pressure (rain coming)

Dew:
- Heavy dew at dawn = fair weather
- No dew = rain or wind coming

"Red sky at night, sailor's delight":
- Red sunset = dry air West (weather comes from West)
- Red sunrise = dry air East, moisture West (storm coming)

Wind:
- Steady wind = stable
- Gusty, changing = storm
- Wind shift (direction change) = frontal passage

SEASONS:

Woolly Bear Caterpillar (Folklore):
- More black = harsh winter
- More brown = mild winter
- Actually: Age of caterpillar, not winter prediction
- Fun but not reliable

Pine Cones:
- Open = dry weather
- Closed = humid/rain
- React to humidity (hygroscopic)
- Actually somewhat reliable

10. LONG-TERM CAMP SETUP
====================

SITE SELECTION:

Criteria:
- Water (within 1/4 mile, not RIGHT on water)
- Fuel (deadwood abundant)
- Shelter materials
- Food sources nearby
- Drainage (not low spot = flooding)
- Sun exposure (warmth)
- Wind protection
- Defensible (if necessary)

Avoid:
- Dry washes (flash flood)
- Lone trees (lightning)
- Dead trees (widowmakers)
- Animal trails (conflict)
- Insect nesting areas

LAYOUT:

Zones:
1. Sleeping: Uphill, dry, level
2. Cooking: Downwind from sleeping (smoke)
3. Latrine: 200+ feet from water, downhill from camp
4. Water: Access path, purification area
5. Storage: Food cache (away from sleeping - bears)
6. Workshop: Tool making, repairs

Fire Pit:
- Ring of rocks (contains fire)
- Clear area 10 feet (fire break)
- Reflector wall (rocks, logs) - directs heat
- Keep away from shelter (sparks)

WATER MANAGEMENT:

Collection:
- Rain catchment (tarp, gutter)
- Spring (best quality)
- Stream (filter/purify)
- Dew collection (morning)

Storage:
- Food-grade containers
- Covered (prevent contamination)
- Cool, dark storage
- Rotate regularly

Gray Water:
- Used water (washing, cooking)
- Dispose away from camp
- Dig pit, cover daily
- Don't contaminate water source

SANITATION:

Latrine:
- Cat hole (solo, temporary)
- Trench latrine (group, semi-permanent)
- 6-12 inches deep
- Cover after each use
- Move when 3/4 full

Hygiene:
- Wash hands (soap, water)
- Bathing (heat water, sponge bath)
- Dental (chew stick, baking soda)
- Prevent illness (critical long-term)

FOOD STORAGE:

Bear Bag:
- Suspend food 12+ feet high, 6 feet from tree
- Rope over branch, pulley system
- Prevents bears, raccoons
- Required in bear country

Cache:
- Elevated platform (4 posts)
- Roof (weather protection)
- Wire mesh (rodents)
- Store: Food, supplies, ammunition

Root Cellar:
- Underground storage
- Cool, stable temperature
- Humidity control
- Store: Vegetables, canned goods

SECURITY:

Perimeter:
- Clear line of sight
- Trip wires (noise makers)
- Booby traps (if legal/necessary)
- Guard duty (if group)

Concealment:
- Natural materials (blends in)
- No straight lines (unnatural)
- Camouflage netting
- Minimal smoke (smokeless fire techniques)

Escape Routes:
- Multiple exits
- Know terrain
- Cached supplies (alternate locations)
- Rendezvous points (if group)

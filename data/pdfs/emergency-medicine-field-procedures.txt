EMERGENCY FIELD MEDICINE
Procedures Without Hospital Access

⚠️ EXTREME EMERGENCY ONLY - Seek professional help if possible

====================
MEDICAL HIERARCHY
====================

1. PREVENT death (ABC: Airway, Breathing, Circulation)
2. TREAT life-threatening injuries
3. STABILIZE for evacuation
4. MANAGE pain
5. PREVENT infection

Time criticality:
- 3 minutes without air = brain death
- 3 hours without warmth (hypothermia) = death
- 3 days without water = death
- 3 weeks without food = death

====================
SEVERE BLEEDING CONTROL
====================

TOURNIQUET APPLICATION:

When to use:
✓ Limb arterial bleeding uncontrolled by pressure
✓ Multiple casualties (triage)
✓ Partial/complete amputation
✗ Head, neck, torso bleeding (impossible)

Procedure:
1. Place 2-3 inches ABOVE wound (toward heart)
2. NEVER place on joint
3. Commercial tourniquet (CAT, SOFT-T) best
4. OR improvised: Belt, cloth (2" wide minimum)
5. Tighten until bleeding STOPS completely
6. Note time applied (write on tourniquet/patient)
7. Do NOT release (unless >2 hours + evacuation possible)

Improvised Tourniquet:
- Cloth band (cravat, shirt strip) 2-4" wide
- Windlass (stick) to twist tight
- Secure windlass (tape, second cloth)
- Tighten until pulse distal to tourniquet gone

⚠️ Tourniquets cause pain (expect screaming)
⚠️ Limb may be lost (life > limb)
⚠️ Don't remove in field (shock risk)

WOUND PACKING:

For wounds that can't be tourniqueted:
1. Expose wound (cut clothes away)
2. Gauze (preferably hemostatic: QuikClot, Celox)
3. Pack DEEP into wound (use finger/forceps)
4. Fill entire wound cavity
5. Direct pressure 3 minutes
6. Pressure dressing over packed wound

Hemostatic agents:
- QuikClot Combat Gauze (best)
- Celox granules
- OR plain gauze (better than nothing)

PRESSURE DRESSING:

1. Gauze pads over wound
2. Wrap elastic bandage (ACE bandage)
3. TIGHT (should restrict blood flow slightly)
4. Check distal pulse (shouldn't cut off completely)

====================
AIRWAY MANAGEMENT
====================

HEIMLICH MANEUVER (Choking):

Conscious patient:
1. Stand behind
2. Fist below sternum, above navel
3. Other hand over fist
4. Quick upward thrusts
5. Repeat until object expelled

Unconscious patient:
1. Lay supine (on back)
2. CPR chest compressions (may dislodge)
3. If visible, finger sweep (careful - don't push deeper)

RECOVERY POSITION (Unconscious but breathing):

1. Kneel beside patient
2. Extend arm nearest you above head
3. Roll patient toward you
4. Bend top knee (stabilizes)
5. Tilt head back (airway open)
6. Monitor breathing continuously

OROPHARYNGEAL AIRWAY (OPA):

Use: Unconscious patient with airway obstruction
1. Measure: Earlobe to corner of mouth
2. Insert upside down initially
3. Rotate 180° as it passes tongue
4. Flange rests on lips
⚠️ Conscious patient = gag reflex (don't use)

CRICOTHYROTOMY (Surgical Airway):

⚠️ ABSOLUTE LAST RESORT - Patient will die without

Indications:
- Airway completely blocked
- Can't ventilate/intubate
- Facial trauma preventing other airway
- Minutes until death

Procedure:
1. Locate cricothyroid membrane (between thyroid + cricoid cartilage)
   - Adam's apple (thyroid), next hard ring below = cricoid
   - Soft spot between = cricothyroid membrane
2. Stabilize larynx (hold thyroid cartilage)
3. Sterilize if time (alcohol, iodine)
4. Vertical incision through skin (1-2 inches)
5. Horizontal incision through membrane (1cm)
6. Insert tube (endotracheal tube, pen barrel, hollow tube)
7. Secure tube
8. Verify air movement (chest rise)

Improvised tube:
- Ballpoint pen (remove ink cartridge)
- Straw
- IV catheter (large bore)
- Tracheostomy tube (ideal if available)

Complications:
- Bleeding (significant)
- Misplacement (through tracheal wall)
- Tube dislodgement
- Infection

====================
CHEST INJURIES
====================

TENSION PNEUMOTHORAX:

Signs:
- Difficulty breathing (severe)
- Trachea deviated to one side
- Decreased breath sounds one side
- Distended neck veins
- Worsening rapidly

Treatment (Needle Decompression):

⚠️ Can kill if done wrong - only if patient dying

1. Locate 2nd intercostal space (2nd rib space)
2. Midclavicular line (nipple line going up)
3. Affected side (decreased breath sounds)
4. 14-16 gauge needle OR angiocath
5. Insert ABOVE rib (vessels below rib)
6. Advance until "pop" or air rush
7. Remove needle, leave catheter
8. Tape in place
9. Should hear air escaping (good sign)

SUCKING CHEST WOUND (Open Pneumothorax):

Treatment:
1. Occlusive dressing (air/water tight)
   - Commercial chest seal (best)
   - Plastic wrap
   - Vaseline gauze
2. Tape on THREE sides only (flutter valve)
3. Open side allows air OUT but not IN
4. Monitor breathing

FLAIL CHEST:

Signs:
- Paradoxical chest movement
- Multiple rib fractures
- Severe pain

Treatment:
- Pain management
- Support breathing
- Don't splint (old technique, causes pneumonia)
- Evacuation urgent

====================
FRACTURES
====================

OPEN FRACTURE (Bone exposed):

⚠️ High infection risk

1. Stop bleeding (pressure, tourniquet if needed)
2. DO NOT push bone back in
3. Cover with sterile dressing (moist if possible)
4. Splint in position found
5. Antibiotics (if available)
6. Evacuation urgent

CLOSED FRACTURE:

Signs:
- Deformity
- Swelling
- Pain, inability to use
- Crepitus (grinding feeling)

Treatment:
1. Check CSM (Circulation, Sensation, Motor)
   - Pulse distal to injury
   - Can patient feel touch?
   - Can patient move digits?
2. Splint in position of comfort
3. Re-check CSM after splinting
4. Immobilize joints above + below fracture
5. Ice if available (20 min on, 20 off)
6. Elevation

Improvised Splints:
- Boards, sticks, tent poles
- Cardboard (folded for rigidity)
- Rolled newspaper/magazine
- Adjacent limb (buddy taping)

FEMUR FRACTURE (Special):

⚠️ Life-threatening (shock from blood loss)

Traction splinting (ideal):
- Kendrick, Hare, or improvised
- Applies continuous pull
- Reduces muscle spasm
- Decreases internal bleeding

Without traction splint:
1. Stabilize as-is
2. Board splints both sides
3. Urgent evacuation
4. IV fluids if available (shock)

====================
BURNS
====================

CLASSIFICATION:

1st Degree (Superficial):
- Red, painful
- No blisters
- Sunburn-like

2nd Degree (Partial Thickness):
- Blisters
- Very painful
- Red/white appearance

3rd Degree (Full Thickness):
- White/black/charred
- PAINLESS (nerves destroyed)
- Leathery appearance

RULE OF NINES (% Body Surface):
- Head: 9%
- Each arm: 9%
- Chest: 9%
- Abdomen: 9%
- Upper back: 9%
- Lower back: 9%
- Each leg front: 9%
- Each leg back: 9%
- Groin: 1%

⚠️ >15% 2nd degree or >5% 3rd degree = CRITICAL

TREATMENT:

Immediate:
1. Remove from heat source
2. Remove clothing (if not stuck)
3. Cool with water (NOT ice)
   - 10-20 minutes
   - Stop before hypothermia
4. Remove jewelry (swelling coming)

Dressing:
1. Cover with sterile dressing
2. Don't pop blisters (infection risk)
3. Non-adherent dressing (Vaseline gauze)
4. Loose wrapping

DON'T:
- Ice (tissue damage)
- Butter, oils (traps heat)
- Pop blisters
- Tight dressings

Fluid Resuscitation (severe burns):

Parkland Formula:
- 4ml × kg body weight × % burned
- Half in first 8 hours
- Half in next 16 hours
- Example: 70kg person, 20% burned
  * 4 × 70 × 20 = 5600ml total
  * 2800ml first 8 hours
  * 2800ml next 16 hours

⚠️ IV access required (advanced)

====================
SHOCK
====================

TYPES:

Hypovolemic (Blood/fluid loss):
- Bleeding, dehydration
- Most common traumatic

Cardiogenic (Heart failure):
- Heart attack
- Heart can't pump

Distributive (Blood vessel dilation):
- Septic (infection)
- Anaphylactic (allergic)
- Neurogenic (spinal injury)

SIGNS:
- Pale, cool, clammy skin
- Rapid weak pulse
- Rapid shallow breathing
- Anxiety, confusion
- Decreased urine output

TREATMENT:

1. Treat cause:
   - Bleeding = control
   - Dehydration = fluids
   - Allergic = epinephrine

2. Position:
   - Supine (flat on back)
   - Legs elevated 12 inches
   - Exception: Head injury (flat only)

3. Maintain body temperature:
   - Blankets
   - Prevent heat loss

4. Fluids:
   - IV (Ringer's lactate, normal saline)
   - OR oral if conscious (water, ORS)

5. Monitor:
   - Pulse
   - Breathing
   - Mental status
   - Skin color/temp

====================
WOUND CARE
====================

CLEANING:

1. Wash hands (soap, water, or sanitizer)
2. Irrigate wound:
   - Clean water (potable)
   - Pressure (syringe, squeeze bottle)
   - Large volume (1-2 liters)
   - Flush debris out
3. NO hydrogen peroxide (damages tissue)
4. Betadine or chlorhexidine (if available)

CLOSURE:

Primary (Immediate):
- Clean wounds <6 hours old
- No signs infection
- Methods: Sutures, staples, glue, tape

Delayed:
- Contaminated wounds
- >6 hours old
- Leave open, pack with gauze
- Close after 3-5 days if no infection

Suturing (if trained):
1. Anesthetic (lidocaine if available)
2. Needle driver, forceps, scissors
3. Curved needle with suture
4. Simple interrupted stitches
5. 1-2mm from wound edge
6. Tie square knots (not too tight)
7. Space ~5mm apart

Improvised Closure:
- Duct tape strips (wound edges together)
- Butterfly bandages
- Superglue (dermabond = medical cyanoacrylate)
- Hair (yes, actually works - tie across wound)

DRESSING:

1. Sterile gauze directly on wound
2. More gauze for absorption
3. Wrap with bandage (secure)
4. Check daily for infection

INFECTION SIGNS:
- Redness spreading
- Increased pain
- Pus
- Red streaks (lymphangitis)
- Fever
- Swelling

Antibiotic (if available):
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate (Augmentin)
- Cephalexin (Keflex)
- Doxycycline
- OR veterinary equivalent (fish antibiotics)

====================
ANTIBIOTICS (Scavenged)
====================

VETERINARY ANTIBIOTICS:

⚠️ Same as human, different packaging

"Fish Mox" = Amoxicillin 250mg
"Fish Mox Forte" = Amoxicillin 500mg
"Fish Flex" = Cephalexin 250mg
"Fish Flox" = Ciprofloxacin 250mg
"Bird Biotic" = Doxycycline 100mg

Dosing (Adult):
- Amoxicillin: 500mg every 8 hours
- Cephalexin: 500mg every 6 hours
- Ciprofloxacin: 500mg every 12 hours
- Doxycycline: 100mg every 12 hours

Duration: 7-10 days (complete course!)

⚠️ Allergies same as human antibiotics
⚠️ Pregnant women: Avoid doxycycline, cipro

SCAVENGING:

- Pet stores (aquarium section)
- Veterinary clinics
- Farm supply stores
- Online (pre-collapse)

====================
PAIN MANAGEMENT
====================

SCAVENGED MEDICATIONS:

Mild-Moderate Pain:
- Ibuprofen: 400-800mg every 6 hours
- Acetaminophen: 500-1000mg every 6 hours
- Aspirin: 325-650mg every 4-6 hours
- Naproxen: 500mg every 12 hours

Moderate-Severe:
- Tramadol: 50-100mg every 6 hours
- Codeine: 30-60mg every 4 hours
- Hydrocodone: (if found - prescription)

⚠️ Addiction risk with opioids
⚠️ Don't combine multiple (liver damage)
⚠️ Don't exceed max daily dose

NATURAL (Less effective):
- Willow bark (aspirin-like)
- Alcohol (moderate amounts)
- Cannabis (if legal/available)

====================
HYPOTHERMIA
====================

STAGES:

Mild (32-35°C / 90-95°F):
- Shivering
- Confusion
- Fumbling hands

Moderate (28-32°C / 82-90°F):
- Shivering stops
- Severe confusion
- Drowsiness

Severe (<28°C / <82°F):
- Unconscious
- Weak pulse
- Shallow breathing
- Appears dead

TREATMENT:

Mild:
1. Remove wet clothes
2. Dry clothing, blankets
3. Warm drinks (if conscious)
4. Body heat (skin-to-skin)
5. Activity (if able)

Moderate/Severe:
1. Handle GENTLY (rough = cardiac arrest)
2. Horizontal position
3. Insulate from ground
4. Passive rewarming (blankets)
5. NO hot water (causes shock)
6. Monitor breathing/pulse
7. CPR if needed (continue longer than normal - hours!)

⚠️ "Not dead until warm and dead"
⚠️ Successful resuscitation after hours possible

====================
HEAT STROKE
====================

SIGNS:
- Core temp >40°C (104°F)
- Confusion, aggression
- NO sweating (skin dry)
- Rapid pulse
- Seizures possible

TREATMENT (Urgent!):

1. Move to shade
2. Remove excess clothing
3. Cool RAPIDLY:
   - Water immersion (best)
   - Wet towels + fan
   - Ice packs (armpits, groin, neck)
4. Monitor temp (stop at 38°C / 100.4°F)
5. IV fluids (if able)
6. Treat seizures (protect from injury)

⚠️ Can cause death/permanent brain damage
⚠️ Organ failure common

====================
DENTAL EMERGENCIES
====================

TOOTH ABSCESS:

Signs:
- Severe toothache
- Swelling (face, gum)
- Fever
- Pus

Treatment:
1. Antibiotics (amoxicillin or clindamycin)
2. Pain relief (ibuprofen + acetaminophen)
3. Warm salt water rinses
4. Drainage if pointing (sterile needle)

⚠️ Can spread to brain (deadly)

TOOTH EXTRACTION (Last resort):

1. Anesthetic (lidocaine injection if available)
2. Grasp tooth with forceps/pliers
3. Rock back and forth (loosen)
4. Pull straight out
5. Gauze pressure (30 minutes)
6. Antibiotics

LOST FILLING:

Temporary:
- Dental cement (pharmacy)
- OR sugar-free gum
- Clove oil (pain relief)

====================
CHILDBIRTH (Emergency)
====================

If no medical help available:

PREPARATION:
1. Clean area
2. Wash hands thoroughly
3. Sterile gloves if possible
4. Clean towels, blankets
5. Bulb syringe (clear airway)
6. Clamps/ties (umbilical cord)

DELIVERY:
1. Mother in comfortable position
2. Support perineum (reduce tearing)
3. Baby crowning = don't push yet (pant)
4. Head delivers = check for cord around neck
5. If cord around neck = slip over head
6. Support head as shoulders deliver
7. Baby will be slippery (careful)

IMMEDIATE NEWBORN:
1. Suction mouth, then nose
2. Dry and stimulate (rub with towel)
3. Should cry (clears lungs)
4. Keep warm (skin-to-skin with mother)

UMBILICAL CORD:
1. Wait until stops pulsing (1-5 min)
2. Clamp/tie in two places (6 inches apart)
3. Cut between ties (sterile scissors/knife)
4. Leave 2-3 inches attached to baby

PLACENTA:
1. Delivers 5-30 minutes after baby
2. DON'T pull cord
3. Wait for natural delivery
4. Gentle traction only
5. Massage uterus after (prevents bleeding)

POST-DELIVERY BLEEDING:
- Massage uterus (firm ball in abdomen)
- Breastfeed (releases oxytocin)
- If severe: Pack uterus with gauze
⚠️ >500ml blood loss = dangerous

====================
LEGAL/ETHICAL
====================

Good Samaritan Laws:
- Protect rescuers in emergencies
- Vary by jurisdiction
- Generally cover reasonable care
- NOT gross negligence

Consent:
- Conscious patient = ask permission
- Unconscious = implied consent (emergency)
- Children = parent consent (if available)

Scope of Practice:
- Only do what you're trained for
- Emergency = leeway
- Document what you did

Do No Harm:
- First principle
- Intervention must help > hurt
- Sometimes best action = none

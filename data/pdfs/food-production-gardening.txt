1. SOIL PREPARATION & COMPOSTING
====================

SOIL TESTING:

pH Testing:
- DIY: Vinegar test (fizz = alkaline), Baking soda (fizz = acidic)
- Accurate: pH meter or test kit
- Ideal pH: 6.0-7.0 (most vegetables)
- Adjust: Lime (raise pH), Sulfur (lower pH)

Texture Test:
- Squeeze handful of moist soil
- Sand: Falls apart (drains fast, low nutrients)
- Clay: Forms tight ball (drains slow, dense)
- Loam: Forms ball but crumbles (IDEAL)

Worm Test:
- Dig 1 cubic foot
- Count worms
- 10+ = healthy soil
- 0-5 = poor soil

COMPOSTING:

Brown (Carbon):
- Dry leaves
- Straw, hay
- Sawdust (untreated wood)
- Cardboard, newspaper
- Dead plants

Green (Nitrogen):
- Grass clippings (fresh)
- Kitchen scraps (no meat/dairy)
- Coffee grounds
- Manure (aged, not fresh)

Ratio: 30:1 Carbon to Nitrogen (roughly 2/3 brown, 1/3 green)

Method:
1. Layer browns and greens
2. Keep moist (like wrung sponge)
3. Turn weekly (aeration)
4. Heat to 130-150°F (kills seeds, pathogens)
5. 2-6 months = finished compost

Hot vs Cold:
- Hot: Turned, fast (2-3 months), kills seeds
- Cold: Unturn, slow (6-12 months), simple

Troubleshooting:
- Smells bad: Too wet, too much green (add brown, turn)
- Not heating: Too dry, too much brown (add green, water)
- Flies: Cover food scraps with brown layer

AMENDMENTS:

Nitrogen:
- Blood meal
- Fish emulsion
- Manure (chicken, rabbit, cow - AGED)
- Compost
- Legume cover crops (nitrogen fixation)

Phosphorus:
- Bone meal
- Rock phosphate
- Wood ash (also potassium)

Potassium:
- Wood ash
- Kelp meal
- Greensand

Calcium:
- Crushed eggshells
- Lime
- Bone meal
- Gypsum

NO-TILL METHOD:

Benefits:
- Preserves soil structure
- Maintains microorganisms
- Less labor
- Improves over time

Technique:
1. Cover crop (winter)
2. Mow/crimp cover crop (spring)
3. Add compost (2-4 inches)
4. Plant directly
5. Mulch heavily (6-8 inches)
6. Repeat annually

Mulch:
- Straw (NOT hay - seeds)
- Wood chips (aged, not fresh)
- Shredded leaves
- Cardboard + straw (sheet mulching)

RAISED BEDS:

Advantages:
- Better drainage
- Warmer soil (earlier planting)
- Easier access (less bending)
- Control soil quality
- Higher yields

Construction:
- Untreated wood (cedar, locust - rot resistant)
- OR stone, concrete blocks
- 4 feet wide (reach center from sides)
- 8-12 inches deep minimum
- Orient North-South (sun exposure)

Soil Mix:
- 1/3 compost
- 1/3 peat moss or coco coir
- 1/3 vermiculite or perlite
- OR: "Mel's Mix" (Square Foot Gardening)

2. SEED SAVING & STORAGE
====================

SEED SAVING:

Open-Pollinated vs Hybrid:
- Open-Pollinated: Breeds true (save seeds)
- Hybrid (F1): Won't breed true (buy new seeds)
- Heirloom: Open-pollinated, 50+ years old

Self-Pollinating (Easy):
- Tomatoes, peppers, beans, peas, lettuce
- Isolate varieties minimally (10 feet)
- Save from best plants

Cross-Pollinating (Harder):
- Cucumbers, squash, melons, corn
- Isolate 1/4 mile OR hand-pollinate
- Save from multiple plants (genetic diversity)

Collection:

Dry Seeds (beans, lettuce, flowers):
1. Let pods/seed heads dry on plant
2. Collect when brown, brittle
3. Separate seeds from chaff
4. Dry further (paper bag, 1-2 weeks)

Wet Seeds (tomatoes, cucumbers):
1. Scoop seeds + gel
2. Ferment in water (3-5 days)
3. Good seeds sink, bad float
4. Rinse clean seeds
5. Dry on screen/paper

STORAGE:

Conditions:
- Cool (40-50°F ideal)
- Dry (<10% moisture)
- Dark
- Airtight containers

Containers:
- Glass jars with tight lids
- Mylar bags (oxygen absorbers)
- Sealed plastic containers
- Desiccant packets (silica gel)

Longevity (Properly stored):
- 1-2 years: Onions, parsnips, corn
- 3-4 years: Beans, peas, carrots, lettuce
- 5+ years: Tomatoes, cucumbers, melons

Testing Viability:
- Paper towel test (germination test)
- 10 seeds between moist paper towels
- Keep warm (70°F)
- Count germination after 7-10 days
- 70%+ = good

SEED BANKS:

Personal Seed Bank:
- Collect annually
- Grow variety (genetic diversity)
- Store multiples (backup)
- Label clearly (variety, year)

Community Seed Sharing:
- Seed swaps
- Seed libraries
- Barter/trade

Commercial Sources (Heirloom):
- Baker Creek Heirloom Seeds
- Seed Savers Exchange
- Johnny's Selected Seeds
- Victory Seeds

3. VEGETABLE GARDENING
====================

PLANNING:

Crop Rotation:
- Don't plant same family same place
- Prevents disease/pest buildup
- 4-year rotation ideal

Families:
- Nightshades: Tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, potatoes
- Brassicas: Cabbage, broccoli, kale, radish
- Cucurbits: Squash, cucumber, melon
- Legumes: Beans, peas (nitrogen fixers)
- Alliums: Onions, garlic, leeks

Example Rotation:
1. Year 1: Nightshades
2. Year 2: Brassicas
3. Year 3: Legumes
4. Year 4: Cucurbits
5. Repeat

Companion Planting:

Good Combinations:
- Tomatoes + Basil (flavor, pest control)
- Corn + Beans + Squash ("Three Sisters")
- Carrots + Onions (pest confusion)
- Marigolds + Anything (pest repellent)

Bad Combinations:
- Tomatoes + Potatoes (same diseases)
- Onions + Beans (stunts growth)
- Fennel + Anything (allelopathic)

CALENDAR (Northern Hemisphere):

Early Spring (March-April):
- Direct sow: Peas, lettuce, spinach, radishes
- Transplant: Onions, cabbage

Late Spring (May):
- Direct sow: Beans, corn, squash, cucumbers
- Transplant: Tomatoes, peppers (after last frost)

Summer (June-July):
- Succession planting: Lettuce, beans, carrots
- Direct sow: Fall crops (late July)

Fall (August-September):
- Direct sow: Lettuce, spinach, kale, radishes
- Garlic planting (October)

Winter:
- Indoor seed starting (February-March)
- Plan next year's garden

HIGH-YIELD CROPS:

Calorie Dense:
- Potatoes: 15,000+ lbs/acre
- Sweet Potatoes: High yield, stores well
- Beans (dried): Protein, stores years
- Squash: Stores 6+ months
- Corn: Staple grain

Nutrient Dense:
- Kale: Vitamins A, C, K
- Tomatoes: Vitamin C, lycopene
- Carrots: Vitamin A
- Beans: Protein, iron
- Garlic: Medicinal

Easy to Grow:
- Lettuce: Fast (30 days)
- Radishes: Fast (25 days)
- Beans: Low maintenance
- Squash: Vigorous growth
- Tomatoes: High yield

GROWING NOTES:

Tomatoes:
- Indeterminate (vine, staking) vs Determinate (bush)
- Prune suckers (increases fruit size)
- Water deeply, regularly (prevent blossom end rot)
- Harvest when fully colored

Potatoes:
- Plant seed potatoes (not grocery store)
- Hill soil as they grow (tubers form in mounded soil)
- Harvest 2 weeks after plants die
- Cure in dark, cool place (2 weeks)

Beans:
- Bush (compact) vs Pole (climbing, higher yield)
- Nitrogen fixers (good for soil)
- Pick regularly (more production)
- Save dried beans (protein storage)

Squash:
- Needs pollinators (bees)
- Powdery mildew common (good airflow helps)
- Harvest winter squash when stem corky
- Cure 2 weeks (hardens skin, stores longer)

Garlic:
- Plant October (hard neck) or November (soft neck)
- Harvest when 50% leaves brown
- Cure 2-3 weeks (hang in shade)
- Stores 6-12 months

4. GRAIN PRODUCTION
====================

WHEAT:

Types:
- Hard Red Winter: Bread flour
- Soft White: Pastry flour
- Hard Red Spring: High protein
- Durum: Pasta

Planting:
- Fall (winter wheat) or Spring (spring wheat)
- Broadcast or drill
- 1-1.5 bushels per acre
- Light cover with soil (rake)

Harvest:
- When heads droop, grain hard
- Cut stalks near ground
- Bundle into shocks (dry 1-2 weeks)
- Thresh (separate grain from straw)
- Winnow (separate chaff from grain)

Threshing Methods:
- Flail (beat stalks)
- Stomp (tarp, walk on it)
- Animal trampling (historic)

Winnowing:
- Pour grain in wind (chaff blows away)
- OR fan + tarp

Storage:
- Dry completely (<10% moisture)
- Airtight containers
- Cool, dark, dry
- Food-grade buckets with oxygen absorbers
- Lasts decades

CORN:

Types:
- Field Corn (dent): Drying, cornmeal
- Sweet Corn: Fresh eating
- Popcorn: Popping
- Flint Corn: Long storage

Planting:
- After last frost (soil 60°F+)
- Blocks (not single rows) for pollination
- 1 foot apart, rows 30 inches
- 1-2 inches deep

Pollination:
- Wind pollinated
- Tassels (male) release pollen
- Silks (female) catch pollen
- Each silk = one kernel

Harvest:
- Sweet corn: Milky stage (squeeze kernel)
- Field corn: Kernels hard, husks dry

Drying:
- Hang ears (husks back) in dry place
- OR dry in sun
- Shell when fully dry (kernels pop off)

Uses:
- Cornmeal (grind)
- Hominy (soak in lye/lime water)
- Masa (nixtamalized corn for tortillas)

OATS:

Planting:
- Early spring (cold tolerant)
- Broadcast 2-3 bushels/acre
- Cover lightly

Harvest:
- When oats golden, straw still green (bottom)
- Cut, bundle, shock
- Thresh and winnow

Processing:
- Hull must be removed (oat groats)
- Difficult without machinery
- Rolled oats = steamed and rolled groats

RICE (If climate suitable):

Types:
- Paddy rice: Flooded fields
- Upland rice: Rain-fed

Planting:
- Paddy: Flood field, broadcast seed
- Upland: Drill or broadcast, rain only

Harvest:
- When heads droop, grain hard
- Cut, dry, thresh, winnow

Processing:
- Brown rice: Hull removed
- White rice: Bran removed (loses nutrition)

5. FRUIT & NUT TREES
====================

APPLES:

Varieties:
- Choose for your climate zone
- Disease resistance important
- Early, mid, late season (extended harvest)

Planting:
- Bare root (dormant) or container
- Dig hole 2x root ball
- Don't plant too deep (graft above soil)
- Stake if needed

Pollination:
- Most need cross-pollination (2 varieties)
- Some self-fertile (check variety)
- Bees essential

Pruning:
- Central leader OR open center
- Remove dead, diseased, crossing branches
- Thin fruit (6 inches apart)
- Prune dormant season (late winter)

Harvest:
- When easily separates from tree
- Twist and lift (don't pull)

Storage:
- Cool (32-40°F), humid
- Varieties vary (some store 6+ months)
- Check regularly, remove bad apples

NUTS:

Chestnuts:
- Fast growing (nuts in 3-5 years)
- High carbohydrates
- Require 2 varieties (pollination)

Walnuts:
- Black walnut (native, hardy)
- English walnut (milder flavor)
- 5-7 years to nuts
- Large trees (plan space)

Hazelnuts:
- Shrub-like (8-12 feet)
- 3-4 years to nuts
- Multiple plants for pollination

Acorns (Oak):
- Native, abundant
- Must leach tannins
- White oak (less tannins) vs Red oak (more)

GRAFTING:

Why:
- Preserve variety
- Faster production
- Multiple varieties one tree

Whip & Tongue (common):
1. Match scion (cutting) to rootstock diameter
2. Cut matching diagonal cuts
3. Cut tongues (interlocking)
4. Join tightly
5. Wrap with grafting tape
6. Seal with grafting wax

Timing:
- Late winter/early spring (dormant)
- OR summer (budding)

BERRY BUSHES:

Blueberries:
- Acidic soil (pH 4.5-5.5)
- 2+ varieties (better yield)
- 3 years to full production
- Netting (birds love them)

Raspberries/Blackberries:
- Summer vs Fall bearing
- Spreads (can be invasive)
- Prune canes after fruiting

Grapes:
- Vines (need trellis)
- Prune heavily (90% of growth)
- 3-4 years to production
- Wine, raisins, fresh eating

6. ANIMAL HUSBANDRY
====================

CHICKENS:

Breeds:
- Layers: Leghorn, Rhode Island Red (eggs)
- Dual purpose: Plymouth Rock, Orpington
- Meat: Cornish Cross (fast growing)

Housing:
- 3-4 sq ft per bird (coop)
- 10 sq ft per bird (run)
- Roost bars (2-3 feet high)
- Nest boxes (1 per 3-4 hens)
- Ventilation (ammonia buildup)

Feeding:
- Commercial feed (16-18% protein layers)
- OR grain mix + protein source
- Grit (digestion)
- Oyster shell (calcium for shells)
- Kitchen scraps supplement

Egg Production:
- Hens lay ~250 eggs/year
- Peak: 1-3 years old
- Light needed (14+ hours)
- Decrease in winter

Meat:
- Butcher: 16-20 weeks (dual purpose)
- 8-12 weeks (Cornish cross)
- Pluck or skin
- Gut, clean, cool immediately

RABBITS:

Advantages:
- Small space
- Quiet
- Fast reproduction
- Efficient meat production (4:1 feed ratio)

Breeds:
- New Zealand White (meat)
- Californian (meat)
- Flemish Giant (large)

Housing:
- Wire cages (droppings fall through)
- 30"×30"×18" per adult
- Nest box for babies

Breeding:
- Sexual maturity: 5-6 months
- Gestation: 30-31 days
- Litter: 6-10 kits
- Wean: 6-8 weeks
- Can breed year-round

Butchering:
- 8-12 weeks old (fryers)
- 5-6 lbs live weight
- Cervical dislocation or blunt force
- Skin, gut, cool
- Tastes like chicken (white meat)

GOATS:

Purpose:
- Dairy: Milk
- Meat: Boer breed
- Fiber: Angora (mohair)

Breeds:
- Nigerian Dwarf: Small, milk
- Nubian: Large, high butterfat milk
- Boer: Meat

Feed:
- Browsers (prefer weeds, brush)
- Hay + browse
- Minerals (free choice)
- Grain supplement (milking)

Milk:
- Dairy goats: 1-2 gallons/day
- Milking 2x daily
- Pasteurize (home consumption safer)

Fencing:
- Goats escape artists
- 4-5 feet high
- Electric fence works
- "Goat proof" nearly impossible

PIGS:

Advantages:
- Eat anything (omnivores)
- Fast growth (6 months to butcher)
- Large amount of meat

Breeds:
- Yorkshire: Common, lean
- Duroc: Marbled meat
- Berkshire: Heritage, flavorful

Housing:
- Shelter from sun, rain
- Wallow (cooling)
- Strong fencing (root under fences)

Feeding:
- Grain mix
- Kitchen scraps
- Garden waste
- Forage (if space)
- 700-800 lbs feed to butcher

Butchering:
- 220-280 lbs (6-8 months)
- Difficult (hire processor often)
- Smoking, curing (bacon, ham)

7. AQUACULTURE & FISH FARMING
====================

TILAPIA:

Advantages:
- Fast growing
- Tolerates poor water
- Vegetarian diet
- Mild flavor

Tank:
- 250-1000 gallons
- 1 lb fish per 5-10 gallons (stocked heavily)
- Filtration critical
- Aeration

Water:
- Temperature: 75-85°F (optimal)
- pH: 6.5-9
- Change 10-25% weekly

Feeding:
- Commercial tilapia pellets
- OR grain-based diet
- Duckweed (grows on pond, free food)

Growth:
- Fingerling to 1 lb: 6-8 months

Breeding:
- Easy (too easy, overpopulate)
- Separate males/females (control)

CATFISH:

Pond Culture:
- 1/4 acre pond (minimum)
- 1000-1500 fish per acre
- Feeding: Floating pellets
- Harvest: Seine net or drain pond

BLUEGILL/SUNFISH:

Combination with Bass:
- Bluegill breed prolifically
- Bass eat small bluegill (population control)
- Harvest bass (bluegill recover quickly)

CRAYFISH:

Pond:
- Shallow (2-3 feet)
- Muddy bottom (burrow)
- Vegetation

Feeding:
- Omnivorous scavengers
- Minimal feed needed

Harvest:
- Traps (baited)
- Boil like shrimp

AQUAPONICS:

System:
- Fish tank + grow beds
- Fish waste → Bacteria → Nitrates → Plants
- Plants filter water → Back to fish
- Symbiotic system

Components:
- Fish tank (100+ gallons)
- Grow beds (gravel or clay pellets)
- Water pump (circulate)
- Air pump (oxygenate)

Fish:
- Tilapia (hardy)
- Goldfish (ornamental but edible)
- Trout (if cool climate)

Plants:
- Leafy greens (lettuce, kale)
- Herbs
- Tomatoes, peppers (heavier feeders)

Balance:
- 1 lb fish per 5-7 gallons water
- 1 sq ft grow bed per 1 gallon fish tank

8. PEST CONTROL
====================

PREVENTION:

Crop Rotation:
- Breaks pest cycles
- Different crops = different pests

Companion Planting:
- Marigolds: Repel aphids, beetles
- Nasturtiums: Trap crop for aphids
- Herbs: Confuse pests (strong scent)

Row Covers:
- Floating row cover (lightweight fabric)
- Keeps insects off plants
- Remove for pollination (or hand pollinate)

Healthy Soil:
- Healthy plants resist pests
- Compost, nutrients
- Avoid stress (water, temperature)

PHYSICAL CONTROLS:

Hand Picking:
- Beetles, caterpillars, slugs
- Drop in soapy water
- Time consuming but effective

Barriers:
- Copper tape (slugs, snails)
- Diatomaceous earth (cuts soft-bodied insects)
- Collars (cutworms)

Traps:
- Beer traps (slugs)
- Yellow sticky traps (aphids, whiteflies)
- Pheromone traps (specific pests)

BIOLOGICAL CONTROLS:

Beneficial Insects:
- Ladybugs: Eat aphids
- Lacewings: Eat aphids, mites
- Praying mantis: Eat anything
- Parasitic wasps: Lay eggs in pests

Attract Beneficials:
- Flowering plants (nectar)
- Dill, fennel, yarrow
- Avoid pesticides (kill beneficials too)

Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt):
- Bacteria toxic to caterpillars
- Organic, safe for beneficial
- Spray on plants

Nematodes:
- Microscopic worms
- Kill soil-dwelling pests
- Apply to moist soil

ORGANIC PESTICIDES:

Neem Oil:
- Broad spectrum (insects, fungus)
- Spray on plants
- Relatively safe

Insecticidal Soap:
- Kills soft-bodied insects (aphids, mites)
- Contact killer (must spray directly)
- Rinse vegetables before eating

Diatomaceous Earth (DE):
- Fossilized algae (sharp edges)
- Cuts insect exoskeleton
- Dust on plants, soil
- Food-grade only (pool DE toxic)

Pyrethrin:
- From chrysanthemum flowers
- Quick knockdown
- Breaks down fast (sun)

ANIMAL PESTS:

Deer:
- 8 foot fence (only real solution)
- Motion-activated sprinklers
- Dogs (guard dogs)

Rabbits:
- 2 foot fence (bury 6 inches)
- Chicken wire around garden

Birds:
- Netting (berries)
- Scarecrows (move regularly)
- Reflective tape

Moles/Gophers:
- Traps (most effective)
- Castor oil (repellent)
- Vibrating stakes (debated)

9. SEASON EXTENSION
====================

COLD FRAMES:

Construction:
- Box with transparent lid
- South-facing
- Glass or plastic top
- Insulated sides (straw bales, soil)

Use:
- Extends season 4-6 weeks (both ends)
- Grow greens through winter (mild climates)
- Harden off seedlings

Ventilation:
- Open lid on warm days (overheat)
- Automatic openers available

HOT BEDS:

Concept:
- Cold frame + heat source
- Historically: Fresh manure (generates heat)
- Modern: Electric heat cable

Heat Source:
- 4-6 inches fresh manure (bottom)
- 6-8 inches soil (top)
- Manure composts, generates heat (60-75°F)
- Lasts 4-6 weeks

CLOCHES:

Types:
- Glass bell jars (traditional)
- Plastic jugs (cut bottom off)
- Wall-o-Water (water-filled tubes)

Use:
- Individual plant protection
- Early planting (2-3 weeks)
- Remove when warm enough

ROW COVERS:

Floating Row Cover:
- Lightweight fabric
- Lays directly on plants
- Allows light, water
- Insulation (+2-8°F)

Hoops:
- PVC or wire hoops
- Row cover over top
- More space (taller plants)
- Walk-in hoop house (larger)

GREENHOUSES:

Types:
- Attached (lean-to) - uses house heat
- Freestanding - separate structure
- High tunnel - simple, unheated
- Hoop house - PVC frame, plastic

Heating:
- Passive solar (thermal mass)
- Compost (heat from decomposition)
- Active (electric, propane) - expensive

Cooling/Ventilation:
- Automatic vent openers
- Shade cloth (summer)
- Fans (air circulation)

Year-Round:
- Greens (lettuce, spinach, kale) through winter
- Warm season crops (tomatoes, peppers) earlier

ROOT CELLARING:

In-Ground Storage:
- Dig pit (below frost line)
- Layer vegetables in sand/straw
- Cover with soil, straw
- Vent pipe (air circulation)

Basement Cellar:
- Coolest corner
- Humidity control (bucket of water)
- Ventilation
- Store: Potatoes, carrots, beets, apples

Conditions:
- 32-40°F (root crops)
- 40-50°F (squash)
- High humidity (80-95%) except squash

10. FOOD FORESTS & PERMACULTURE
====================

PERMACULTURE PRINCIPLES:

1. Observe and Interact
2. Catch and Store Energy
3. Obtain a Yield
4. Self-Regulation and Feedback
5. Use Renewable Resources
6. Produce No Waste
7. Design from Patterns to Details
8. Integrate Rather Than Segregate
9. Small and Slow Solutions
10. Use and Value Diversity
11. Use Edges and Value the Marginal
12. Creatively Respond to Change

FOOD FOREST LAYERS:

1. Canopy: Large fruit/nut trees (apples, walnuts)
2. Understory: Dwarf fruit trees (peach, plum)
3. Shrub: Berry bushes (blueberry, currant)
4. Herbaceous: Perennial vegetables (asparagus, rhubarb)
5. Groundcover: Strawberries, clover
6. Rhizosphere: Root crops (potatoes, Jerusalem artichoke)
7. Vertical: Vines (grapes, kiwi)

Benefits:
- Mimics natural forest
- Self-maintaining (minimal input)
- Biodiversity (pest resistance)
- Perennial (replant rarely)

GUILD PLANTING:

Example: Apple Tree Guild
- Canopy: Apple tree
- Nitrogen fixers: Clover, comfrey (beneath)
- Pest deterrents: Chives, nasturtium
- Pollinators: Borage, yarrow
- Mulch: Comfrey (chop and drop)

Synergy:
- Each plant supports others
- Reduced maintenance
- Increased yield

WATER MANAGEMENT:

Swales:
- Contour ditches (on-contour)
- Catch water, slow flow
- Infiltration (recharge groundwater)
- Plant on downhill side (water available)

Hugelkultur:
- Buried wood beds
- Logs on bottom, soil on top
- Wood absorbs water (irrigation)
- Decomposes = nutrients
- Raised bed (improved drainage)

Rain Catchment:
- Roof gutters → tanks
- First flush diverter (clean water)
- Overflow to swales/garden
- Gravity-fed irrigation

COMPOSTING SYSTEMS:

Worm Bins (Vermicomposting):
- Red wigglers (worms)
- Kitchen scraps → worm castings
- Liquid fertilizer (worm tea)
- Indoor/outdoor

Chickens:
- Movable coops (chicken tractor)
- Till and fertilize area
- Move to new area
- Rotation with planting

Compost Piles:
- Multiple bins (rotation)
- Turn regularly OR passive
- Harvest bottom (finished compost)

PERENNIAL VEGETABLES:

Asparagus:
- 20+ year lifespan
- Harvest spring (shoots)
- 2-3 years to first harvest

Rhubarb:
- Tart stalks (leaves toxic)
- Harvest spring
- Divide every 5-10 years

Jerusalem Artichoke:
- Invasive spreader
- Edible tubers
- Harvest fall/winter

Walking Onions:
- Produces bulbs on top (falls, replants)
- Self-propagating
- Harvest anytime

Horseradish:
- Pungent root
- Spreads aggressively
- Harvest fall

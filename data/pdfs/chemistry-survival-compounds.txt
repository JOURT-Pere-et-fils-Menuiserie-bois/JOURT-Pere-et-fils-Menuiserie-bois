1. SOAP PRODUCTION
====================

COLD PROCESS METHOD:

Ingredients:
- Fat/Oil (animal fat, vegetable oil)
- Lye (Sodium Hydroxide - NaOH)
- Water

LYE CREATION (From wood ash):

1. COLLECT HARDWOOD ASH
   - Oak, hickory, maple (best)
   - Pine, softwoods (less effective)
   - Burn completely (white ash)
   - Collect ~10 cups ash

2. LEACH POTASH
   - Container with small holes in bottom
   - Fill with straw/grass (filter)
   - Add wood ash
   - Pour water through (1:1 ratio)
   - Collect brown liquid = lye solution

3. CONCENTRATION
   - Boil lye solution
   - Reduce volume by half
   - Test: Float fresh egg
   - Should float = ready
   - Too weak = boil more

SOAP MAKING:

Traditional Method:
1. Heat fat/oil (40-50°C)
2. Slowly add lye solution
3. Stir continuously (30-60 min)
4. "Trace" appears (pudding-like)
5. Pour into molds
6. Cure 4-6 weeks

⚠️ LYE IS CAUSTIC:
- Severe burns on contact
- Wear protection (gloves, goggles)
- Vinegar neutralizes lye burns
- NEVER add water to lye (explosive boil)
- Always add lye to water

Soap Calculator:
- Different fats need different lye amounts
- Beef tallow: SAP value 0.140
- Olive oil: SAP value 0.134
- Coconut oil: SAP value 0.178

Formula: (Fat weight × SAP value) × 0.95 = NaOH needed

LIQUID SOAP (Potassium Hydroxide):
- Wood ash lye = KOH (potassium)
- Creates liquid/gel soap
- Easier than NaOH process
- Dilute with water after cooking

2. ALCOHOL PRODUCTION
====================

ETHANOL (Drinking/Disinfectant/Fuel):

FERMENTATION:

Requirements:
- Sugar source (fruit, grain, honey)
- Yeast (commercial or wild)
- Water
- Anaerobic environment

Simple Mash:
1. Sugar/grain + water (1:4 ratio)
2. Heat to 65°C (starch conversion if grain)
3. Cool to 20-25°C
4. Add yeast
5. Seal container (airlock ideal)
6. Ferment 7-14 days
7. Bubbling stops = ready

Sugar Sources:
- Table sugar (easiest)
- Honey
- Fruit juice
- Grain (requires mashing)
- Potatoes (starch → sugar conversion)

Wild Yeast:
- Grape skins (white film = yeast)
- Apple skins
- 50/50 success (can spoil instead)
- Commercial yeast more reliable

DISTILLATION:

⚠️ ILLEGAL in many countries without license

Simple Still:
1. Heat source (fire, electric)
2. Pot (fermented mash)
3. Collection tube/coil (condenser)
4. Cooling method (water jacket)
5. Collection container

Process:
1. Heat mash to 78.4°C (ethanol boiling point)
2. Vapor rises through tube
3. Condenser cools vapor → liquid
4. Collect distillate

FRACTIONS:
- First 50ml (per 5L mash) = METHANOL (POISON!)
- Discard foreshots (heads)
- Collect "hearts" (ethanol)
- Stop when temp >90°C (tails)

Purity:
- First distillation: 40-60% alcohol
- Second distillation: 80-90%
- Third: 90-95%
- Azeotrope limit: 95.6% (water/ethanol inseparable)

SAFETY:
🔥 HIGHLY FLAMMABLE (vapor + heat = explosion)
☠️ Methanol = BLINDNESS/DEATH
⚚ Distill outside or well-ventilated
🧪 Never sealed system (pressure explosion)

Uses:
- Disinfection (70% most effective)
- Drinking (diluted, no methanol)
- Fuel (stoves, lamps)
- Solvent
- Trade/barter

====================
3. ACIDS & BASES
====================

ACETIC ACID (Vinegar):

Fermentation Method:
1. Ethanol solution (wine, cider, beer)
2. Acetobacter bacteria (mother of vinegar)
3. Aerobic environment (oxygen needed)
4. 1-3 months fermentation
5. Result: 4-8% acetic acid

Uses:
- Food preservation (pickling)
- Cleaning
- pH adjustment
- Weak acid reactions

CITRIC ACID:

Source: Lemons, limes (6-8% citric acid)
1. Juice citrus fruits
2. Strain pulp
3. Use directly or dry
4. Crystallization (evaporate, cool)

Uses:
- Preservative
- Flavor
- Cleaning (descaling)
- Chelating agent

SULFURIC ACID (Advanced):

⚠️ EXTREMELY DANGEROUS

Old Battery Method (Dilute H2SO4):
- Car battery electrolyte = ~30% H2SO4
- Drain battery (CAREFULLY)
- Use dilute for reactions
- NEVER concentrate (explosive)

Lead Chamber Process (Industrial):
- SO2 (burn sulfur) + NO2 + H2O
- Too dangerous/complex for field

HYDROCHLORIC ACID:

Salt + Sulfuric Acid Method:
- NaCl + H2SO4 → HCl (gas) + NaHSO4
- Bubble HCl through water
- Dangerous (corrosive gas)

Uses:
- pH control
- Cleaning (muriatic acid)
- Chemical synthesis

SODIUM HYDROXIDE (Lye):

See soap section above (wood ash leaching)

Commercial:
- Drain cleaner (pure NaOH)
- Very caustic

Uses:
- Soap making
- Biodiesel production
- pH adjustment
- Dissolving proteins/fats

CALCIUM HYDROXIDE (Slaked Lime):

Production:
1. Burn limestone/seashells (CaCO3)
2. → Quicklime (CaO) + CO2
3. Add water (exothermic!)
4. → Ca(OH)2 (slaked lime)

Uses:
- Mortar/cement
- Tanning leather
- Nixtamalization (corn → hominy)
- Disinfectant (whitewash)

====================
4. DISINFECTANTS
====================

BLEACH (Sodium Hypochlorite):

Pool Shock Method:
- Calcium hypochlorite (pool chemical)
- More stable than liquid bleach
- 1 tsp per gallon water = disinfectant
- 1/4 tsp per gallon = drinking water

DIY (Electrolysis):
- Salt water + electricity
- Produces NaClO + H2 gas
- Dangerous (chlorine gas, hydrogen)
- Requires knowledge

IODINE:

Sources:
- Betadine (povidone-iodine)
- Tincture of iodine (alcohol solution)
- Kelp/seaweed (extraction complex)

Water Purification:
- 5 drops per liter
- Wait 30 minutes
- Taste improvement: Vitamin C after

HYDROGEN PEROXIDE:

Concentration:
- 3% (pharmacy) - Wound cleaning
- 6% (salon) - Hair bleaching
- 35% (industrial) - DANGEROUS

Stability:
- Decomposes to water + oxygen
- Store cool, dark
- Brown bottle preferred

Uses:
- Wound disinfection
- Surface sterilization
- Bleaching
- Oxidizer (rocket fuel if concentrated!)

ALCOHOL:

See ethanol section
- 70% most effective (not 100%)
- Evaporates (not residual)
- Flammable

====================
5. PRESERVATIVES
====================

SALT:

Dry Curing:
- Cover meat completely
- 3-4 weeks
- Removes moisture
- Prevents bacterial growth

Brining:
- 20-25% salt solution
- Submerge meat
- Weight to keep under
- 1-3 weeks

SUGAR:

Preservation:
- High concentration (jams, jellies)
- >50% sugar prevents bacteria
- Osmotic pressure

VINEGAR:

Pickling:
- 5% acetic acid minimum
- Boil vegetables in vinegar
- Seal in jars
- Lasts years

SMOKING:

Compounds:
- Phenols (antibacterial)
- Formaldehyde (preservative)
- Drying effect

Cold Smoke: 20-30°C (preserves, doesn't cook)
Hot Smoke: 60-80°C (cooks + preserves)

NITRITES/NITRATES:

Sources:
- Saltpeter (KNO3)
- Celery (natural nitrates)

Curing:
- Pink salt (#1 or #2)
- Prevents botulism
- Creates cured flavor/color

⚠️ Excess nitrites = toxic
⚠️ Follow recipes precisely

6. MEDICATIONS (Natural)
====================

ASPIRIN (Salicylic Acid):

Willow Bark Method:
1. Harvest willow bark (spring best)
2. Dry completely
3. Powder finely
4. Boil in water (tea)
5. Contains salicin → salicylic acid in body

Dosage: 1-2 tsp bark powder per dose
Effects: Pain relief, anti-inflammatory, fever reduction

QUININE (Antimalarial):

Source: Cinchona bark
- Grows in specific regions (South America, Indonesia)
- Boil bark in water
- Bitter tea
- Treats malaria

⚠️ Difficult to dose precisely
⚠️ Can cause cinchonism (overdose)

ANTIBIOTICS:

Penicillin (Mold):
- Penicillium mold (bread, citrus)
- Growing is easy, purification nearly impossible
- NOT recommended (impure = dangerous)

Natural Antibacterials:
- Honey (wound dressing)
- Garlic (allicin)
- Tea tree oil (topical)
- Colloidal silver (debated efficacy)

⚠️ NOT substitutes for real antibiotics
⚠️ Use fish/vet antibiotics if needed

ANESTHETICS:

Alcohol:
- Crude but effective (high doses)
- Not surgical anesthesia
- Pain dulling only

Opium Poppy:
- Papaver somniferum
- Latex contains morphine/codeine
- Score seed pod, collect latex
- HIGHLY ADDICTIVE
- Illegal most places

⚠️ Overdose = respiratory arrest
⚠️ Legal issues severe

7. FUELS
====================

BIODIESEL:

Transesterification:
1. Vegetable oil/animal fat
2. Methanol (wood alcohol)
3. Lye (NaOH or KOH catalyst)

Process:
1. Heat oil (50-60°C)
2. Mix methanol + lye (25% oil volume methanol)
3. Add to oil, mix 1 hour
4. Separate layers (biodiesel top, glycerin bottom)
5. Wash biodiesel (water)
6. Dry completely

Ratio: 100L oil + 20L methanol + 400g lye → ~100L biodiesel

⚠️ Methanol highly toxic
⚠️ Process produces flammable vapors

METHANOL (Wood Alcohol):

Destructive Distillation:
1. Heat wood without oxygen (retort)
2. Vapor condenses
3. Separate fractions
4. Toxic but useful (fuel)

⚠️ NEVER DRINK (blindness/death)

CHARCOAL:

Production:
1. Burn wood in low-oxygen environment
2. Dirt mound method (cover burning wood)
3. OR metal barrel with vent hole
4. Cook until smoke stops
5. Cool completely

Uses:
- Fuel (cooking, heating)
- Filtration (activated charcoal)
- Blackpowder component
- Drawing/marking

====================
SAFETY PRECAUTIONS
====================

GENERAL:
- Ventilation critical (toxic vapors)
- Eye protection always
- Gloves for caustics
- Fire extinguisher nearby
- First aid kit accessible

LABELING:
- Mark ALL containers
- Date created
- Contents
- Concentration
- Warnings

STORAGE:
- Cool, dark, dry
- Separate incompatibles
- Acids away from bases
- Oxidizers away from fuels

DISPOSAL:
- Neutralize before discarding
- Acid + base = neutral salt + water
- Dilute and dispose (small amounts)
- Never down drain (large amounts)

TESTING:
- Small batches first
- Test before scaling up
- Document what works

1. FIREARMS FUNDAMENTALS
====================

BASIC FIREARM TYPES:

Handguns:
- Revolver: 5-8 rounds, reliable, slower reload
- Semi-auto Pistol: 10-20 rounds, faster reload
- Calibers: 9mm, .45 ACP, .40 S&W, .38 Special

Rifles:
- Bolt-action: Accurate, slow fire rate
- Semi-auto: Moderate fire rate (AR-15, AK-47)
- Lever-action: Classic, tubular magazine
- Calibers: .223/5.56, 7.62×39, .308/7.62 NATO, .22 LR

Shotguns:
- Pump-action: Reliable, 5-8 shells
- Semi-auto: Faster, more complex
- Break-action: Simple, 1-2 shells
- Gauges: 12ga (most common), 20ga, .410

FOUR RULES OF GUN SAFETY:

1. Treat every gun as if loaded
2. Never point at anything you don't want to destroy
3. Keep finger OFF trigger until ready to shoot
4. Be sure of target and what's beyond it

MARKSMANSHIP FUNDAMENTALS:

Sight Alignment:
- Front sight focused (rear blurry)
- Top of front sight level with rear
- Equal light on both sides
- Target slightly blurry

Sight Picture:
- Aligned sights on target
- Focus on front sight
- Target center mass (combat)
- Bullseye (precision)

Trigger Control:
- Straight back pressure
- Surprise break (don't anticipate)
- Smooth, continuous pull
- Reset (semi-auto)

Breathing:
- Natural respiratory pause
- Inhale, exhale partially, pause, shoot
- OR shoot between breaths
- Don't hold breath (shake)

Stance:
- Isosceles: Square to target, arms extended
- Weaver: Bladed stance, push-pull tension
- Modern: Aggressive forward lean
- Stable base, knees slightly bent

DRAW (Pistol):

4-Count Draw:
1. Grip (establish firing grip)
2. Clear (draw from holster)
3. Rotate (level pistol, finger on trigger)
4. Extend (full extension, sight alignment)

Speed: 1-2 seconds (trained)
Accuracy more important than speed

RELOADS:

Tactical Reload:
- Still rounds in magazine
- Replace with full magazine
- Retain partial magazine

Emergency Reload:
- Magazine empty, slide locked
- Drop empty magazine
- Insert new magazine
- Release slide (chamber round)
- Get back on target

Speed Reload:
- Magazine empty
- Drop magazine (don't retain)
- Slam in new magazine
- Rack slide
- Fastest method

MALFUNCTION CLEARANCE:

Type 1 (Failure to Fire):
- "Tap, Rack, Bang"
- Tap magazine (ensure seated)
- Rack slide (eject round, chamber new)
- Assess target, fire if needed

Type 2 (Stovepipe):
- Case stuck in ejection port
- Sweep away with hand
- Rack slide
- Ready

Type 3 (Double Feed):
- Two rounds jammed
- Lock slide back
- Rip magazine out
- Rack slide 3 times (clear)
- New magazine
- Rack slide
- Ready

2. IMPROVISED WEAPONS
====================

BLUNT INSTRUMENTS:

Baseball Bat:
- Effective reach
- Two-hand grip
- Swing for head/knees
- Wood or aluminum

Pipe/Club:
- 18-24 inches ideal
- Cap one end (weight)
- Two-hand or one-hand
- Aim for head, joints

Tactical Flashlight:
- Heavyweight (>200g)
- Strike with bezel
- Temporary blindness (strobe)
- Dual purpose (light + weapon)

EDGED WEAPONS:

Kitchen Knife:
- 6-8 inch blade
- Fixed blade better than folder
- Reverse grip (icepick) for power
- Forward grip for reach/slashing

Improvised Knife:
- Sharpened metal/glass
- Wrapped handle (tape, cloth)
- Broken bottle (desperate)
- Sharpened screwdriver

Axe/Hatchet:
- Devastating power
- Requires skill
- Training essential
- Lethal force

PROJECTILE WEAPONS:

Slingshot:
- Y-frame (wood, metal)
- Latex tubing
- Pouch (leather)
- Ammo: Steel balls, rocks
- Accurate to 30m with practice

Bow & Arrow:
- Recurve or longbow (DIY possible)
- Arrows: Straight wood, fletched, pointed
- Draw weight: 40-60 lbs hunting
- Silent, reusable ammo
- Skill intensive

Crossbow:
- Mechanical advantage
- Hold drawn (no muscle fatigue)
- More accurate than bow (less skill)
- Slower reload
- DIY difficult but possible

Spear:
- 6-8 foot pole
- Sharpened/pointed end
- OR knife lashed to pole
- Thrust don't throw (retain weapon)
- Reach advantage

THROWING WEAPONS:

Rocks/Projectiles:
- Fist-sized rocks
- Overhand throw
- Aim for head
- Distraction or damage

Molotov Cocktail:
- Glass bottle
- Gasoline + oil/tar (thickener)
- Cloth wick
- Light and throw
- Area denial, anti-vehicle
- ⚠️ Dangerous to user

DEFENSIVE TOOLS:

Pepper Spray (OC):
- 10-12 foot range
- Aim for face
- Cause temporary blindness, pain
- Wind awareness (blowback)
- Legal most places

Taser/Stun Gun:
- Electric shock
- Close range (contact)
- Taser (projectile) = distance
- Not always effective (thick clothing)
- Training required

Kubaton:
- 5.5 inch metal rod
- Keychain weapon
- Pressure points
- Strikes to nerve clusters
- Legal most places

3. HAND-TO-HAND COMBAT
====================

TARGET AREAS:

High Value:
- Eyes (gouge, poke)
- Throat (strike, choke)
- Groin (kick, knee, strike)
- Knees (kick, stomp)
- Nose (palm strike, headbutt)

Vulnerable Points:
- Temple (side of head)
- Jaw (knockout potential)
- Solar plexus (wind knocked out)
- Floating ribs (fracture)
- Instep (stomp)

STRIKES:

Punch:
- Make tight fist (thumb outside)
- Strike with first two knuckles
- Rotate hip for power
- Straight punch faster than haymaker

Palm Strike:
- Base of palm (heel of hand)
- Upward to nose/chin
- Won't break hand (vs punch)
- Effective and safer

Elbow Strike:
- Short range, devastating
- Horizontal, vertical, reverse
- Very hard surface
- Multiple angles

Knee Strike:
- Clinch enemy
- Pull down while kneeing up
- Target groin, solar plexus, face
- Brutal close-range weapon

Kicks:
- Front kick (push kick)
- Roundhouse (power kick)
- Side kick (penetration)
- Groin kick (low, fast)
- ⚠️ Risk of being caught/falling

GRAPPLING:

Chokes:
- Rear naked choke (RNC): Under chin, bicep & forearm compress carotid
- Guillotine: Front headlock, arm under chin
- Triangle: Legs around neck (ground)
- Unconscious in 8-10 seconds
- ⚠️ Can kill if held too long

Escapes:
- Create distance
- Strike to create opening
- Don't go to ground (multiple attackers)
- Stand up ASAP

Takedowns:
- Double leg (tackle)
- Single leg
- Throw (judo)
- ⚠️ Only if 1-on-1

WEAPONS DEFENSE:

Knife Defense:
- Create distance (priority #1)
- Use barriers (chair, jacket)
- Expect to get cut
- Control weapon hand
- Strike with other hand
- Disarm if possible
- RUN if able

Gun Defense:
- ONLY if gun contact distance
- Redirect muzzle (offline)
- Control weapon
- Strike attacker
- Disarm
- ⚠️ Last resort, high risk

Club Defense:
- Close distance (inside swing arc)
- Block with forearm
- Strike vulnerable areas
- Disarm/control weapon

MULTIPLE ATTACKERS:

Tactics:
- Don't get surrounded
- Keep moving
- Position attackers behind each other
- Strike and move
- Create obstacles
- ESCAPE priority (not fight)

Target Priority:
- Closest threat first
- Armed before unarmed
- Aggressive before passive

4. TACTICAL MOVEMENT
====================

MOVEMENT TECHNIQUES:

Individual Movement:
- Low Crawl: Flat on ground, slow, concealed
- High Crawl: Hands and knees, faster, less concealed
- Rush: 3-5 seconds sprint, hit ground, observe
- Walking: Heel-toe or toe-heel (silent)

Team Movement:
- Traveling: Moderate speed, moderate security
- Traveling Overwatch: One element covers while other moves
- Bounding Overwatch: One element stationary (cover) while other bounds forward

COVER vs CONCEALMENT:

Cover:
- Stops bullets
- Brick wall, concrete, engine block, thick wood
- Earth (2-3 feet minimum)
- Steel plate

Concealment:
- Hides you but doesn't stop bullets
- Bushes, tall grass, darkness
- Drywall, car doors, wooden fence
- Plywood, thin metal

Critical: KNOW THE DIFFERENCE

USE OF COVER:

Positions:
- Shoot around (not over if possible)
- Minimize exposure
- Alternate positions (don't pop up same spot)
- Avoid silhouetting

Angles:
- Stand back from cover (see more, harder to hit)
- Pie the corner (gradual reveal)
- Dominant eye side (right-handers peek right)

BUILDING ENTRY:

⚠️ EXTREMELY DANGEROUS - Team activity

Principles:
- Speed, Surprise, Violence of Action
- Clear corners immediately
- Don't silhouette in doorway (fatal funnel)
- "Slice the pie" (gradual entry)
- Communicate constantly

Solo Entry (Avoid if possible):
- Pie the door from outside
- Quick peek (if time)
- Dynamic entry or slow clear
- Clear immediate threats first
- Check corners (where you can't see)

ROOM CLEARING:

4-Man Team (Military):
1. #1: Enters first, clears corner
2. #2: Opposite corner
3. #3: Follows #1
4. #4: Follows #2
- Clear center, far corners
- Verbal "Clear!" when done

2-Man Team:
- #1 left, #2 right
- Cross and clear
- More dangerous (fewer guns)

Solo:
- Very dangerous
- Avoid if possible
- Pie from outside
- Quick decisive entry
- ⚠️ Easy to get ambushed

STAIRWELLS:

Going Up:
- Extremely dangerous (high ground disadvantage)
- Pie each landing
- Move quickly when committed
- Check above constantly

Going Down:
- Easier (high ground advantage)
- Still pie corners
- Don't skyline on stairs

5. DEFENSIVE POSITIONS
====================

HOME DEFENSE:

Hardening:
- Solid core doors
- Deadbolts (Grade 1)
- Strike plates with 3" screws
- Window locks/bars
- Motion lights
- Alarm system

Safe Room:
- Interior room, no windows
- Solid door with deadbolt
- Phone/radio
- Weapons
- First aid
- Food/water for 24 hours

Response Plan:
- Gather family to safe room
- Call police
- Defend safe room (don't clear house)
- Stay put until police arrive

Fields of Fire:
- Know your home layout
- Identify choke points
- Fatal funnel (doorways)
- Cover positions
- Backdrop (what's behind target)

PERIMETER DEFENSE:

Layered Defense:
1. Outer: Fencing, lighting, visibility
2. Middle: Alarms, sensors, cameras
3. Inner: Locks, barriers, safe room
4. Last: Armed response

Observation:
- 360° awareness
- Dead space elimination
- Clear fields of view
- Remove hiding spots (trim bushes)

Early Warning:
- Dogs (best alarm)
- Motion sensors
- Cameras (visible + hidden)
- Trip wires (non-explosive alarms)

FIGHTING POSITIONS:

Hasty Position:
- Natural cover used quickly
- Rock, tree, ditch, vehicle
- Minimal preparation
- Temporary

Improved Position:
- Dug in, sandbags
- Overhead cover
- 18-24 inches thick earth
- Fire step, grenade sump

Range Card:
- Sketch of area
- Reference points
- Distances
- Dead space
- Fields of fire

6. SMALL UNIT TACTICS
====================

FIRE TEAM (4 people):

Roles:
- Team Leader: Commands, rifleman
- Rifleman: Standard weapon
- Automatic Rifleman: LMG/SAW
- Grenadier: Rifle + grenade launcher

Formation:
- Wedge: General purpose
- File: Trails, close terrain
- Line: Maximum firepower forward

SQUAD (8-13 people):

Organization:
- Squad Leader
- 2 Fire Teams (4 each)
- OR 3 Fire Teams (3 each)

Tactics:
- Bounding overwatch
- Fire and maneuver
- Flanking
- Suppression

AMBUSH:

Types:
- Linear (L-shaped): Along road/trail
- V-shaped: Funnel into kill zone
- Circular: Surround (large force)

Execution:
1. Position (concealed, good fields of fire)
2. Wait (patience critical)
3. Initiate (command detonated, first shot)
4. Destroy enemy in kill zone
5. Withdraw (quickly, alternate route)

Linear Ambush Setup:
```
        [Assault Line]
        X X X X X X
            ↓
    Enemy → → → (Kill Zone)
            ↓
        X X X (Support)
```

PATROL:

Types:
- Reconnaissance: Gather information
- Combat: Find and engage enemy
- Security: Protect area

Patrol Formation:
- Point man (lead, observes)
- Slack man (2nd, covers point)
- Main body
- Rear security

Patrol Base:
- Circular perimeter
- 360° security
- Listening posts outside perimeter
- Rest/resupply location

REACT TO CONTACT:

Contact Front:
- Return fire immediately
- Take cover
- Flanking element moves to flank
- Suppress and maneuver

Contact Rear:
- Rear element returns fire
- Leader directs
- May reverse direction or continue forward

Ambush (you're ambushed):
- Assault through ambush (if in kill zone)
- Return fire immediately
- Don't stay in kill zone (fatal)
- Violence of action

7. SURVEILLANCE & EVASION
====================

OBSERVATION POST (OP):

Location:
- Good visibility
- Concealed position
- Multiple escape routes
- Cover from fire

Equipment:
- Binoculars/scope
- Notepad/camera
- Radio
- Camouflage
- Food/water (long duration)

Rotation:
- 2-person minimum (one observes, one rests)
- 4-hour shifts
- Night vision if available

CAMOUFLAGE:

Visual:
- Break up outline (shape)
- Match colors (environment)
- Avoid shine (cover skin, equipment)
- Movement discipline (slow, minimal)

Patterns:
- Woodland: Green, brown, black
- Desert: Tan, brown, light green
- Urban: Gray, black, white
- Snow: White, light gray

Application:
- Face paint (diagonal stripes, not horizontal)
- Vegetation (local, fresh)
- Netting
- Ghillie suit (advanced)

TRACKING:

Signs:
- Footprints (direction, age, number)
- Broken vegetation (height, direction)
- Displaced rocks/leaves
- Trash/equipment dropped
- Smell (smoke, cologne, food)

Aging:
- Fresh: Sharp edges, moist
- Hours: Edges softening
- Days: Weathered, filled with debris
- Weeks: Nearly invisible

Counter-Tracking:
- Hard surfaces (rock, pavement)
- Walk in water (streams)
- Backtracking (walk backwards, confuse)
- False trails
- Brush out tracks (time-consuming)

EVASION:

Principles:
- Avoid contact
- Travel at night
- Use terrain (ravines, thick vegetation)
- Minimize traces
- Irregular schedule

Rest Areas:
- Off main route
- Concealed
- Multiple escape routes
- Avoid obvious (caves, buildings)

Water:
- Necessary but dangerous (people congregate)
- Observe before approaching
- Fill quickly, leave
- Alternative: dew collection, rain

Food:
- Lower priority than evasion
- Foraging/hunting creates traces
- Fires visible/smells
- Cache supplies if possible

8. MARKSMANSHIP
====================

ZEROING:

Process:
1. Secure rifle (sandbag, rest)
2. Fire 3-round group at known distance
3. Measure deviation from point of aim
4. Adjust sights (clicks or MOA)
5. Repeat until centered

Zero Distances:
- Rifle: 100 yards (common), 25/300m (military)
- Pistol: 25 yards
- Shotgun: Pattern test (25 yards)

MOA (Minute of Angle):
- 1 MOA = ~1 inch at 100 yards
- 2 inches at 200 yards
- Scope adjustment in 1/4 MOA clicks typically

RANGE ESTIMATION:

Techniques:
- Mil-dots (reticle markings)
- Known size method: Range = (Size × 1000) / Mils
- Bracketing (known references)
- Laser rangefinder (if available)

Holds:
- 100m: Center mass
- 200m: Top of head
- 300m: 1 mil above head
- Adjust for caliber/ballistics

WIND READING:

Indicators:
- Flags, grass movement
- Mirage (heat shimmer)
- Trees swaying
- Feel on face

Effect:
- Full value: 90° to bullet path
- Half value: 45° to bullet path
- No value: Directly toward/away

Correction:
- Hold into wind
- Or adjust scope
- Practice essential

SHOOTING POSITIONS:

Prone:
- Most stable
- Lowest profile
- Legs spread, body angle to target
- Support with magazine/bipod

Sitting:
- Moderately stable
- Elbows on knees
- Cross-legged or open-legged

Kneeling:
- Quick to assume
- Less stable
- Support elbow on knee
- Sling support helps

Standing (Offhand):
- Least stable
- Fastest
- Elbow supported on ribs
- Breathing critical

SNAP SHOOTING:

Technique:
- Quick target acquisition
- Minimal aiming (point shooting)
- Close range (<25m)
- Both eyes open
- Instinctive

Practice:
- Dry fire (empty weapon)
- Quick draw to shot
- Multiple targets
- Move and shoot

9. WEAPON MAINTENANCE
====================

CLEANING:

Frequency:
- After every use (ideal)
- Weekly if carried daily
- Monthly if stored

Process:
1. Ensure unloaded (check twice!)
2. Disassemble (field strip)
3. Brush barrel (bore brush + solvent)
4. Patch until clean (white patch)
5. Lightly oil all metal
6. Wipe excess oil
7. Reassemble
8. Function check

Solvents:
- Hoppes #9 (common)
- CLP (clean, lubricate, protect)
- Break-Free
- OR: Kerosene, diesel (improvised)

Lubricants:
- Gun oil
- CLP
- Mobile 1 synthetic (improvised)
- Avoid: WD-40 (not a lubricant)

MALFUNCTIONS:

Failure to Feed:
- Weak magazine spring
- Dirty chamber
- Bad ammunition
- Bent feed lips

Failure to Eject:
- Weak extractor
- Dirty chamber
- Weak ammo (underpowered)

Failure to Fire:
- Bad primer (ammo)
- Firing pin broken/dirty
- Light striker spring

Stovepipe:
- Weak ejector
- Weak ammo
- Dirty/damaged extractor
- Limp wristing (pistol)

IMPROVISED MAINTENANCE:

Cleaning:
- Motor oil (gun oil substitute)
- Gasoline (solvent)
- Toothbrush (brush)
- Cloth patches (cut t-shirt)
- Bore snake (paracord + cloth)

Lubrication Points:
- Slide rails (pistol)
- Bolt carrier (rifle)
- Trigger group (sparingly)
- Barrel locking lugs

Storage:
- Clean and oiled
- Dry environment
- Desiccant (silica gel)
- Inspect monthly
- Cycle ammunition (brass degrades)

10. BALLISTICS & AMMUNITION
====================

TERMINAL BALLISTICS:

Factors:
- Velocity: Faster = more damage
- Mass: Heavier = deeper penetration
- Expansion: Hollow point = more damage
- Construction: FMJ vs soft point

Wound Channel:
- Permanent cavity: Bullet path
- Temporary cavity: Tissue displacement
- Fragmentation: Increases damage
- Hydrostatic shock (debated)

Overpenetration:
- FMJ tends to overpenetrate
- Hollow point expands, stops sooner
- Barrier penetration (walls, glass)
- Consideration for home defense

COMMON CALIBERS:

Pistol:
- 9mm: Standard, effective, common
- .45 ACP: Larger, slower, "stopping power"
- .40 S&W: Middle ground
- .38 Special: Revolver, mild recoil
- .357 Magnum: Powerful revolver round
- .22 LR: Weak but cheap, quiet, available

Rifle:
- .223/5.56: AR-15, low recoil, fragmentation
- 7.62×39: AK-47, moderate power
- .308/7.62 NATO: Full power, long range
- .30-06: Classic hunting, powerful
- .22 LR: Training, small game
- 6.5 Creedmoor: Long range precision

Shotgun:
- 12 gauge: Most common, powerful
- 20 gauge: Less recoil, effective
- .410: Very light, limited power

BULLET TYPES:

FMJ (Full Metal Jacket):
- Military standard
- Penetrates well
- Doesn't expand
- Overpenetrates tissue

JHP (Jacketed Hollow Point):
- Self-defense standard
- Expands on impact
- More damage
- Less penetration

Soft Point:
- Hunting
- Moderate expansion
- Good penetration
- Balanced

AP (Armor Piercing):
- Hardened core (steel, tungsten)
- Penetrates armor
- Illegal for civilian use (most countries)

BALLISTIC TABLES:

5.56 NATO (55gr):
- Muzzle: 3200 fps
- 100y: 2770 fps, -1.5" drop
- 200y: 2360 fps, -7" drop
- 300y: 1980 fps, -20" drop

7.62×39 (123gr):
- Muzzle: 2350 fps
- 100y: 2080 fps, -3" drop
- 200y: 1830 fps, -13" drop
- 300y: 1600 fps, -35" drop

9mm (115gr):
- Muzzle: 1200 fps
- 25y: 1100 fps, -1" drop
- 50y: 1030 fps, -5" drop

RELOADING (Ammunition):

⚠️ Precision required - Mistakes can explode gun

Components:
1. Brass case (reusable)
2. Primer (small/large, rifle/pistol)
3. Powder (specific type, precise weight)
4. Bullet (projectile)

Process:
1. Inspect brass (cracks, splits)
2. Decap (remove old primer)
3. Resize (full length or neck)
4. Prime (new primer)
5. Charge (powder - PRECISE)
6. Seat bullet (correct depth)
7. Crimp (optional)

Equipment:
- Single stage press (beginner)
- Progressive press (high volume)
- Dies (caliber specific)
- Powder measure
- Scale (digital, precise)
- Calipers

Safety:
- Never exceed max load
- Single powder type at bench
- Double-check every charge
- Wear safety glasses
- Start low, work up

====================
AMMUNITION STORAGE
====================

Conditions:
- Cool (below 70°F ideal)
- Dry (humidity <50%)
- Dark (UV degrades powder)
- Sealed (ammo cans, desiccant)

Shelf Life:
- Factory: 10+ years (proper storage)
- 50+ years documented (still fires)
- Primers degrade first
- Inspect regularly

Rotation:
- FIFO (First In, First Out)
- Date ammunition
- Shoot old stock (training)
- Replenish with fresh

====================
LEGAL CONSIDERATIONS
====================

⚖️ VARIES DRASTICALLY BY JURISDICTION

Firearms:
- USA: 2nd Amendment, state laws vary
- Background checks (federal)
- Concealed carry permits (state)
- NFA items (suppressors, SBR, full-auto)

Self-Defense Laws:
- Castle Doctrine: No duty to retreat (home)
- Stand Your Ground: No duty to retreat (public)
- Duty to Retreat: Must attempt escape first
- Reasonable force: Proportional response
- Imminent threat: Immediate danger

Use of Force Continuum:
1. Presence
2. Verbal commands
3. Empty hand control
4. Less-lethal (taser, OC)
5. Lethal force

⚠️ Lethal force ONLY when:
- Imminent threat of death/serious injury
- No other reasonable option
- Proportional to threat
- Immediately stop when threat ends

Post-Incident:
- Call police immediately
- State you defended yourself
- Request attorney
- DO NOT give detailed statement without lawyer
- Expect arrest (possible)
- Legal fees (tens of thousands)

International:
- EU: Very restricted (varies by country)
- UK: Essentially banned (handguns)
- Australia: Heavily restricted
- Canada: Restricted, licensing required
- Switzerland: High ownership, well regulated
- Check local laws (CRITICAL)

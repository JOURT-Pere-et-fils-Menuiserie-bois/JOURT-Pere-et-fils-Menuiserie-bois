1. MEASUREMENT CONVERSIONS
====================

LENGTH:
1 inch (in) = 2.54 centimeters (cm)
1 foot (ft) = 12 inches = 30.48 cm
1 yard (yd) = 3 feet = 91.44 cm
1 mile = 5280 feet = 1.609 kilometers (km)
1 meter (m) = 3.281 feet = 39.37 inches
1 kilometer = 0.621 miles

AREA:
1 acre = 43,560 square feet
1 acre = 4047 square meters
1 hectare = 2.471 acres
1 square mile = 640 acres

VOLUME:
1 gallon (US) = 4 quarts = 8 pints = 128 fl oz
1 gallon = 3.785 liters
1 liter = 0.264 gallons = 1000 milliliters
1 cup = 8 fl oz = 236.6 ml
1 tablespoon (tbsp) = 3 teaspoons (tsp) = 15 ml
1 teaspoon = 5 ml

WEIGHT:
1 pound (lb) = 16 ounces (oz) = 453.6 grams (g)
1 ounce = 28.35 grams
1 kilogram (kg) = 2.205 pounds = 1000 grams
1 ton (US) = 2000 pounds
1 metric ton (tonne) = 1000 kg = 2205 pounds

SPEED:
1 mph = 1.609 km/h = 0.447 m/s
1 km/h = 0.621 mph
1 knot = 1.151 mph = 1.852 km/h

PRESSURE:
1 PSI = 6.895 kilopascals (kPa)
1 bar = 14.5 PSI = 100 kPa
1 atmosphere = 14.7 PSI = 101.3 kPa

ENERGY:
1 calorie = 4.184 joules
1 kilocalorie (food calorie) = 1000 calories = 4184 joules
1 BTU = 1055 joules = 252 calories
1 kilowatt-hour (kWh) = 3.6 million joules

2. TEMPERATURE SCALES
====================

CONVERSION FORMULAS:
°C = (°F - 32) × 5/9
°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
K = °C + 273.15

QUICK REFERENCE:
-40°F = -40°C (same!)
0°F = -17.8°C
32°F = 0°C (water freezes)
50°F = 10°C
68°F = 20°C (room temp)
98.6°F = 37°C (human body temp)
100°F = 37.8°C
212°F = 100°C (water boils at sea level)

SURVIVAL TEMPERATURES:
Hypothermia begins: <95°F (35°C) core temp
Normal body: 97-99°F (36-37°C)
Fever: >100.4°F (38°C)
Dangerous fever: >103°F (39.4°C)
Hyperthermia (heat stroke): >104°F (40°C)

WATER:
Freezing point: 32°F / 0°C / 273K
Boiling point (sea level): 212°F / 100°C / 373K
Boiling decreases: -1.8°F per 1000 ft elevation
                   -1°C per 300 m elevation

FOOD SAFETY:
Danger Zone: 40-140°F (4-60°C) bacteria multiply
Cold storage: <40°F (4°C)
Freezer: 0°F (-18°C) or below
Hot holding: >140°F (60°C)
Cooking (most foods): >165°F (74°C)

ENVIRONMENTAL:
Comfortable room: 68-72°F (20-22°C)
Ideal sleep: 60-67°F (15-19°C)
Heatstroke risk: >105°F (40°C) ambient
Frostbite risk: <32°F (0°C) with wind

4. WATER REQUIREMENTS
====================

DAILY WATER NEEDS:
Minimum survival: 1 liter/day
Temperate climate: 2-4 liters/day
Hot climate: 4-10 liters/day
Heavy work: +2-4 liters/day
Breastfeeding: +1 liter/day

ACTIVITIES (water loss per hour):
Resting in shade: 0.3 liters
Light work: 0.6 liters
Moderate work: 1.0 liters
Heavy work: 1.5 liters

DEHYDRATION SYMPTOMS:
1-2% body weight loss: Thirst, decreased performance
3-5%: Dry mouth, fatigue, headache
6-8%: Dizziness, confusion, difficulty walking
>10%: Delirium, unconsciousness
>15%: Death

URINE COLOR CHART:
Clear/Pale yellow: Well hydrated
Yellow: Adequately hydrated
Dark yellow: Mild dehydration
Brown/Amber: Severe dehydration (medical emergency)

WATER PURIFICATION TIME:
Boiling (rolling boil):
- Sea level: 1 minute
- 5000 ft (1500m): 2 minutes
- 10000 ft (3000m): 3 minutes

Bleach (5.25% sodium hypochlorite):
- Clear water: 8 drops per gallon, wait 30 min
- Cloudy water: 16 drops per gallon, wait 30 min

Iodine tablets:
- 1-2 tablets per liter, wait 30 minutes

SODIS (Solar disinfection):
- Clear PET bottle in direct sun: 6 hours

5. FIRST AID DOSAGES
====================

⚠️ GENERAL DOSING - Verify with packaging

PAIN/FEVER (Adult):
Ibuprofen:
- Dose: 200-400 mg every 4-6 hours
- Max: 1200 mg/day (OTC), 3200 mg/day (prescription)

Acetaminophen (Tylenol):
- Dose: 325-650 mg every 4-6 hours
- Max: 3000 mg/day (liver damage risk)

Aspirin:
- Dose: 325-650 mg every 4 hours
- Max: 4000 mg/day

ALLERGIC REACTION:
Diphenhydramine (Benadryl):
- Adult: 25-50 mg every 4-6 hours
- Child: 1 mg per kg, max 50 mg
- Max: 300 mg/day

Epinephrine (EpiPen):
- Adult: 0.3 mg IM (intramuscular)
- Child (15-30 kg): 0.15 mg IM
- Can repeat after 5-15 minutes if needed

ANTIBIOTICS (If available):

Amoxicillin:
- Adult: 500 mg every 8 hours, 7-10 days
- Child: 25 mg/kg/day divided into 2-3 doses

Doxycycline:
- Adult: 100 mg twice daily
- Child >8 years: 2 mg/kg twice daily
- Do NOT give children <8 (tooth staining)

Ciprofloxacin:
- Adult: 500 mg twice daily
- Not for children (joint issues)

⚠️ Complete full antibiotic course (don't stop early)

WOUND CARE:
Hydrogen Peroxide: Dilute 3% solution (kills bacteria but delays healing)
Iodine (Betadine): 10% solution, dilute for wounds
Antibiotic Ointment: Apply thin layer, cover

REHYDRATION SOLUTION (ORS):
- 1 liter clean water
- 6 teaspoons sugar
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- Mix well, drink slowly

6. KNOT TYING GUIDE
====================

BASIC KNOTS:

Square Knot (Reef Knot):
Use: Join two ropes (same diameter)
Warning: Slips under load, not for critical use
Mnemonic: "Right over left, left over right"

Bowline:
Use: Fixed loop at end of rope
Benefits: Won't slip, easy to untie
"Rabbit comes out of hole, around tree, back in hole"

Clove Hitch:
Use: Attach rope to pole/tree
Quick: Two loops over object
Warning: Can slip, back up with half-hitch

Taut-Line Hitch:
Use: Adjustable loop (tent guy-lines)
Benefits: Slides along rope, grips under load

Sheet Bend:
Use: Join two ropes (different diameters)
Benefits: More secure than square knot

Trucker's Hitch:
Use: Mechanical advantage for tightening
Creates 3:1 pulley system
Secure loads, tighten tarps

Figure-8 Follow-Through:
Use: Climbing, safety critical
Very strong, won't slip

Prusik Knot:
Use: Slide on rope, grip under load
Climbing, rescue

LASHINGS:

Square Lashing:
Use: Join perpendicular poles (90°)
Applications: Scaffolding, structures

Diagonal Lashing:
Use: Brace two poles at angle
Applications: X-bracing

Tripod Lashing:
Use: Create tripod from 3 poles
Applications: Cooking tripod, shelter

Timing:
Dot: 1 unit

SIGNAL MIRROR:

Aiming:
1. Reflect sun onto nearby object
2. Move reflection to target
3. Flash (move slowly back and forth)

SOS: 3 short, 3 long, 3 short flashes

Effective range: 10+ miles (perfect conditions)

GROUND-TO-AIR SIGNALS:

International Symbols (arrange rocks, logs):

V = Require Assistance
X = Require Medical Assistance
F = Require Food and Water
→ = Proceeding in this direction
⊥ = I (vertical bar with base) = Require engineer/tools

Triangle (equilateral) = Safe to land

Signal Fire:
3 fires in triangle (100 ft apart) = distress

WHISTLE SIGNALS:

1 blast = "Where are you?" OR Stop
2 blasts = "Come to me"
3 blasts = Emergency/Distress

Effective range: 1/2 mile

8. PLANT IDENTIFICATION
====================

LEAF ARRANGEMENTS:

Alternate: One leaf per node, alternating sides
Opposite: Two leaves per node, across from each other
Whorled: Three+ leaves per node, circular

LEAF SHAPES:

Simple Leaves:
- Entire margin (smooth edge)
- Serrated (saw-tooth edge)
- Lobed (oak-like divisions)
- Linear (grass-like)
- Ovate (egg-shaped)
- Lanceolate (spear-shaped)

Compound Leaves:
- Pinnate (feather-like, central stem)
- Palmate (hand-like, from single point)

POISONOUS PLANT FEATURES (Warning signs):

"Leaves of three, let it be" (Poison Ivy/Oak)
Milky sap (often toxic - exception: dandelion)
Umbrella-shaped flowers (many toxic: Hemlock family)
Red berries (50/50 edible/toxic)
White/yellow berries (90% toxic)

EDIBLE WILD PLANT INDICATORS:
Berry aggregates (raspberry-like): Usually safe
Grass family: Seeds generally edible
Oak trees: All produce edible acorns (leach tannins)
Clover: All species edible

⚠️ NEVER eat unless 100% positive ID

9. ANIMAL TRACKS
====================

TRACK MEASUREMENTS:

Whitetail Deer:
- Size: 2-3 inches long
- Shape: Split hoof (two toes)
- Stride: 12-18 inches (walking)
- Sign: Droppings (pellets), rubs on trees

Black Bear:
- Size: 4-7 inches front, 7-9 inches rear
- Shape: 5 toes, claws visible
- Pattern: Pigeon-toed walk
- Sign: Scat (varies by diet), claw marks on trees

Raccoon:
- Size: 2-3 inches
- Shape: 5 toes, looks like human baby hand
- Pattern: Distinctive "hands"
- Sign: Near water, food remnants

Coyote/Wolf:
- Size: Coyote 2.5", Wolf 4"
- Shape: 4 toes + claws, oval (vs. cat round)
- Pattern: Direct register (hind in front track)
- Sign: Scat with hair/bone

Bobcat/Mountain Lion:
- Size: Bobcat 2", Lion 3-4"
- Shape: 4 toes, NO claws visible (retracted)
- Pattern: Round shape
- Sign: Scrape marks, cache prey

Rabbit:
- Size: 1-2 inches
- Shape: 4 toes front, 5 rear
- Pattern: Rear feet in front when hopping
- Sign: Droppings (pellets), chewed vegetation

TRACK AGING:

Fresh (hours):
- Sharp edges
- Moist soil
- Debris intact

Recent (1-2 days):
- Edges softening
- Dry
- Some debris gone

Old (3+ days):
- Eroded edges
- Filled with debris
- Weathered

SCAT IDENTIFICATION:

Herbivore:
- Pellets or formless pile
- Vegetation visible
- Little smell

Carnivore:
- Tubular, twisted
- Hair, bone visible
- Strong smell

Omnivore:
- Variable (depends on diet)
- Seeds, hair mixed
- Raccoon: often near water

10. ASTRONOMY & TIMEKEEPING
====================

TELLING TIME BY SUN:

Shadow Method:
- Shortest shadow = Solar noon (NOT 12pm clock)
- Shadow points North (Northern Hemisphere)
- Each 15° = 1 hour difference from noon

Sundial:
- Vertical stick (gnomon)
- Mark shadow hourly
- Permanent time reference

Hand Method (Sunset estimation):
- Extend arm, hand horizontal
- Count finger-widths from sun to horizon
- Each finger ≈ 15 minutes
- 4 fingers = 1 hour until sunset

NIGHT SKY NAVIGATION:

Polaris (North Star):
- Altitude above horizon = Your latitude
- Example: 45° altitude = 45° North latitude
- Always points North (within 1°)

Big Dipper as Clock:
- Rotates around Polaris
- Full rotation = 24 hours
- Can estimate time by position

Orion (Winter constellation):
- Rises East, sets West
- Belt points to Sirius (brightest star)
- Visible worldwide

Southern Cross (Southern Hemisphere):
- Points to South celestial pole
- Extend long axis 4.5 times
- Drop to horizon = South

MOON PHASES (Northern Hemisphere):

New Moon: Not visible (Sun side)
Waxing Crescent: Right side lit
First Quarter: Right half lit (rises noon, sets midnight)
Waxing Gibbous: More than half lit
Full Moon: Fully lit (rises sunset, sets sunrise)
Waning Gibbous: Left side lit
Last Quarter: Left half lit (rises midnight, sets noon)
Waning Crescent: Thin left crescent

Mnemonic:
- "DOC" in Northern Hemisphere
- D = waxing (bulge on right)
- O = full
- C = waning (bulge on left)

Reverse in Southern Hemisphere

LUNAR MONTH:
- 29.5 days (new moon to new moon)
- 12 lunar months = 354 days (11 days short of solar year)

SEASONS (Northern Hemisphere):

Spring Equinox: ~March 20 (day = night)
Summer Solstice: ~June 21 (longest day)
Fall Equinox: ~September 22 (day = night)
Winter Solstice: ~December 21 (shortest day)

Sun Path:
- Summer: Rises NE, sets NW (high arc)
- Winter: Rises SE, sets SW (low arc)
- Equinox: Rises E, sets W

ESTIMATING REMAINING DAYLIGHT:

Finger Method:
- Extend arm fully
- Measure fingers between sun and horizon
- Each finger ≈ 15 minutes
- 4 fingers = 1 hour

Rule of Thumb:
- Thumb width ≈ 1°
- Fist width ≈ 10°
- Span (thumb to pinky) ≈ 25°

STARS FOR CARDINAL DIRECTIONS:

North: Polaris (Ursa Minor)
South: Southern Cross (Crux)
East: Rising stars (any)
West: Setting stars (any)

PLANETS (Visible to naked eye):
- Venus: Brightest (morning/evening star)
- Jupiter: 2nd brightest
- Mars: Red color
- Saturn: Yellowish
- Mercury: Near horizon (hard to see)

METEOR SHOWERS (Annual):

Perseids: Mid-August (50-100/hour)
Geminids: Mid-December (100-120/hour)
Leonids: Mid-November (variable)

Best viewing:
- After midnight
- Away from city lights
- New moon (darker skies)

====================
QUICK SURVIVAL RULES
====================

RULE OF 3s:
- 3 minutes without air
- 3 hours without shelter (extreme weather)
- 3 days without water
- 3 weeks without food

SURVIVAL PRIORITIES:
1. Shelter (hypothermia kills fast)
2. Water (dehydration kills in days)
3. Fire (warmth, purification, signaling)
4. Food (can survive weeks)
5. Rescue (signaling)

LOST/SURVIVAL:
S.T.O.P.
- Sit down
- Think
- Observe
- Plan

SIGNALING:
3 of anything = Distress
- 3 fires
- 3 whistle blasts
- 3 gunshots
- 3 smoke columns

SHELTER LOCATION (Avoid):
- Dry washes (flash flood)
- Lone trees (lightning)
- Dead trees (widow makers)
- Animal trails (conflict)
- Bottoms of valleys (cold air sinks)

WATER SOURCES:
1. Streams/rivers (flowing)
2. Springs
3. Rain collection
4. Dew collection
5. Vegetation (transpiration bags)

FOOD PROCUREMENT:
1. Foraging (plants, berries)
2. Fishing (easiest animal protein)
3. Trapping (passive hunting)
4. Hunting (skill intensive)

NAVIGATION WITHOUT COMPASS:
1. Sun (rises East, sets West)
2. Stars (Polaris = North)
3. Moss (unreliable)
4. Landmarks
